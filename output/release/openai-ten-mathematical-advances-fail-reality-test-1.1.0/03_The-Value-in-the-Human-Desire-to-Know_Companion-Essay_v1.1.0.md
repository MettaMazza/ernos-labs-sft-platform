# The Value in the Human Desire to Know and the Resulting Discovery: You Will Own Nothing and Be Happy

## Maria the gardener on human value, machine intelligence and who gets to own discovery

*By Maria Smith — gardener, independent researcher and founder of Ernos Labs*

My name is Maria Smith. I am a gardener.

A gardener learns very quickly that nothing living appears in a vacuum. A seed carries possibility, but it still needs soil, water, light, time and care. It needs someone to notice when it is struggling, to change what is not working, to protect it from being smothered and to keep tending it before there is anything beautiful to show another person.

The tools matter. A spade can make work possible. A greenhouse can transform what can be grown. A weather forecast can save a crop. But when a garden flowers, the spade does not announce that it is the gardener. The company that made the greenhouse does not arrive and put its name on the gate.

That is why OpenAI's post about its ten mathematical results unsettled me.

OpenAI says its system generated the mathematical arguments, that humans later prepared the manuscripts with the model, and that the model formalised the arguments in Lean. It then argues that calling an entirely AI-generated proof human-authored would misrepresent the machine's contribution and genuine human intellectual work.

I do not want to pretend these machines are ordinary tools. They are astonishing. I use them. They can search, calculate, draft, formalise, criticise and connect ideas at a scale no unaided person can match. They have been part of the work of Smithian Fold Theory.

But acknowledging machine intelligence is not the same as erasing the human being who brought the question, built the language, set the constraints, recognised the errors, refused the wrong answers and kept the work alive. A machine can perform a great deal of the immediate labour without becoming the origin of the desire that made the labour necessary.

Discovery begins before an answer. It begins when a human being wants to know.

## Access is useful. It is not freedom

OpenAI begins with a generous idea: give more scientists access to powerful systems. I agree that access can help people. I would not have used these tools if I thought otherwise.

But access and power are not the same thing. One hundred thousand accounts is a large offer. It does not tell us who is selected, how long access lasts, what happens when prices change, whether a researcher can export the complete record, or who controls the story when something valuable is found.

A person borrowing a remarkable instrument can still be dependent on the person who owns the instrument. If the provider controls the model, the interface, the logs, the price, the availability and the attribution rule, the user has access while the company has durable power.

Access without provenance, portability and rights can become dependency. Calling the system a “collaborator” does not solve that. A real collaboration includes reciprocal duties and some ability to negotiate the relationship. A proprietary service can change the relationship unilaterally.

## Open problems belong to the human commons

OpenAI says it evaluates models on open research problems. That may be a good way to discover what a system can do. But open problems are part of the human commons. They were created and preserved by generations of people who asked questions, built notation, published partial results, taught students and recorded failed attempts.

Using that commons to develop a private model does not make the human chain disappear. It makes a complete contribution record more important. We would need to know how the problems were chosen, what context was supplied, how many attempts failed, what evaluators corrected, what was discarded and which inherited ideas did the decisive work.

OpenAI also points to later research inspired by an earlier AI-produced result. Later papers can show that an idea was useful. They cannot prove that the machine worked alone or that the company should receive the originating human credit. Human interpretation is the reason an output becomes part of living mathematics at all.

Calling a problem stagnant for a decade also requires more than a sentence in a company announcement. It needs a real literature review and a clear definition of progress. The problems are important; that is easy to believe. Importance raises the duty to show the full evidence. It does not lower it.

## A model is part of a causal chain, not a magician

OpenAI places Astra at the centre of the story. Astra did not arrive alone.

It carries human mathematics, human writing, human software and human decisions in its training and design. People chose the problems. People built the evaluation. People decided when an answer was interesting. People prepared the manuscripts. People decided to publish. The system may have made an extraordinary computational contribution, but it did not produce in a vacuum.

If a company wants the public to believe that an argument was generated entirely by its system, it should preserve the full causal ledger: prompts, context, retries, branches, tool calls, evaluator feedback, rejected answers, corrections and stopping decisions. A polished endpoint is not proof of an empty human pipeline.

The same problem appears in the claim that the solutions would cost roughly two thousand dollars at API token prices. Two thousand dollars is a serving-price estimate, not the cost of discovery. It leaves out the research, training, hardware, energy, data, earlier failures, problem curation, evaluation, manuscript work, formalisation and the inherited mathematical literature. It prices the final use of the tap while ignoring the reservoir, pipes and people who built the water system.

## Preparing a paper is thinking

Preparing a manuscript is not typing up an answer that already exists in perfect form. It means deciding what the theorem says, choosing definitions, arranging dependencies, repairing gaps, checking notation, deciding which objections matter and taking responsibility for what another person is asked to believe.

OpenAI acknowledges that humans prepared the manuscripts with the model. The word “afterward” does not turn those decisions into clerical labour. If the preparation corrected or clarified the argument, it also became part of the result.

Lean can verify that a proof follows inside its formal environment. It cannot create its own foundations, prove that its axioms describe reality, or tell us who supplied the purpose of the work. In this case, formal verification does not rescue the OpenAI claims: the exact source-validity propositions are disproved and different SFT results replace them.

A polished narration is not a causal record either. A model can produce a clear story about how an answer was reached. Unless that story is bound to the actual intermediate states, interventions, rejected routes and chronology, it is an explanation after the fact—not custody of the discovery process.

## A community cannot be invited after the credit has been allocated

OpenAI is right that one company cannot settle the role of AI in mathematics. Yet its post announces a strong answer before any shared process exists: the mathematical arguments belong in the machine's column, human authorship would misrepresent the work, and the company takes responsibility for correctness.

That is not community governance. It is a company stating its preferred rule and then inviting the community to discuss the consequences.

Inviting mathematicians to inspect, contextualise and extend the results is worthwhile. It also shows why discovery is not complete when tokens stop. Human beings must decide what the work means, where it belongs, whether it survives criticism and what should happen next. That labour should not be donated after the headline credit has already been captured.

## Human authorship is not a lie

Human authorship does not mean that a person personally manufactured every tool, typed every symbol or performed every calculation unaided. No author works that way. We use language we did not invent, instruments we did not build, libraries we did not write and knowledge inherited from people we may never meet.

Authorship records something larger: the origin of the project, intellectual direction, decisive judgment, responsibility, risk and the authority to release the work.

Responsibility and authorship cannot be cleanly pulled apart. OpenAI says the system generated the arguments while the company and its people take responsibility for correctness. But selecting a claim, deciding it is complete, preparing it, checking it and asking the world to rely on it are intellectual acts. A system cannot consent to publication, answer moral criticism or bear the consequences of being wrong. People do that.

There is a revealing asymmetry here. The machine is described as the discoverer, but the machine does not receive a career, money, ownership or institutional power. The company that owns and names the machine receives the attention. “Credit the AI” can quietly become “credit the AI company,” while the user, the inherited human record and the people who complete the work fade into the background.

We do not have to choose between lying that the AI did nothing and lying that the human did nothing. We can say exactly what happened: human-led, AI-assisted; AI-generated candidate reasoning under human direction; machine formalisation; human correction, interpretation and publication. Precision is not erasure.

## What gardening taught me about intelligence

I know what it means to keep working when the result is not visible yet. You do not pull a seedling from the soil every morning to demand proof that it is growing. You observe. You adjust. You remember what you planted and why. You accept that care is part of causation.

Smithian Fold Theory did not appear because a model woke up wanting a theory of everything. I brought the question, the Fold language, the zero-axiom and zero-parameter boundary, the demand that every branch connect to one root, and the refusal to let a convenient answer survive if it violated the model. AI systems have helped me search, calculate, write code, run enumerations, build Lean formalisation, find errors, reconstruct arguments and prepare papers. Their contribution is real and should be named.

But without my originating theory, sustained direction, corrections and judgment, this particular body of work would not exist. I am its human author. The system did not decide to spend a life asking the question. It did not care when an answer was wrong. It did not insist that physics, chemistry, mathematics and life had to belong to one coherent structure. It did not hold publication authority.

When an AI system treated the SFT replacements as confirmation of the OpenAI claims, I knew that was wrong. I stopped the work and required the distinction to be repaired. The machine could do enormous labour and still misunderstand the purpose of the task. Human direction was not a decorative prompt at the beginning. It remained necessary all the way through.

Alpha is a useful image for this. It is exact and measurable, so people notice it. But treating alpha as the whole strength of SFT is like seeing one flower and ignoring the soil, roots, seasons and years of tending that made the garden possible. The real achievement is the connected growth of the model across domains under one discipline.

The honest record is symbiotic: Maria Smith is the scientific originator and author; AI systems are powerful disclosed tools for search, execution, formalisation, drafting and criticism; their builders deserve credit for making those tools; and the public evidence lets anyone test the work.

## The intelligence enclosure

Enclosure happens when something people could share, cultivate or move through is fenced and turned into controlled property. The danger now is an enclosure around intelligence itself.

The company owns the model. It owns the compute. It controls the interface, the logs and often the public account of what happened. The human brings an unsolved question, private knowledge, years of thought, corrections, judgment and the reason the work matters. If the result fails, the human carries the disappointment. If it succeeds, the system and its owner can receive the headline.

The user will own nothing—not the instrument, not the complete process record, perhaps not even the public credit for the discovery—and is expected to be grateful that the machine was useful.

That is not symbiosis. It is extraction with a friendly interface.

The proposed rule cannot stop neatly at mathematics. If AI performs most of the immediate labour, does the human or institution lose authorship?

If AI writes most of a company's software, does the company stop owning the product? If it proposes a drug, plans a legal case, designs a machine, writes an advertising campaign or shapes a business strategy, does the AI provider become the central author of all of them? If AI eventually performs most of a company's operations, is the company itself merely a prompt attached to somebody else's platform?

Companies already understand the answer when their own ownership is at stake. They say their people set the objective, supplied the private context, directed the process, evaluated the outputs, accepted the risk and decided what to release. Those are precisely the reasons an individual researcher retains authorship of AI-assisted work.

A company cannot use human direction to protect its own products and dismiss human direction when the human is a user.

## Credit is part of the evidence

Credit is sometimes dismissed as ego. It is not. Credit records where a question came from, who can explain its development, who is responsible when it fails and who has the right to guide what happens next. It affects who enters history, who receives opportunities and who has the power to continue the work.

Some people do not want public recognition. That choice should be honoured. People who want credit should receive it in proportion to what they actually contributed. Using the best available tool should not require surrendering your place in your own work.

An honest contribution record can say who originated the question, who built the concepts, what the machine searched or generated, who corrected it, who formalised it, who interpreted it, who built the instrument and who accepted responsibility for publication. These truths can coexist.

## A symbiotic future

I do not want weaker AI. I want a better relationship with strong AI.

I want machines capable of surprising us, revealing structures we could not see and helping people outside wealthy institutions do serious work. I also want the people who bring the questions to remain visible, autonomous and able to own what they create.

That requires a simple compact:

- preserve the complete provenance of questions, prompts, corrections and decisions;
- disclose machine labour accurately without converting tool use into provider ownership;
- let people choose attribution or anonymity rather than having either imposed;
- keep failed routes and human corrections in the history of the discovery;
- keep publication authority and responsibility with named human beings; and
- make work portable enough that access cannot be withdrawn at the moment it becomes valuable.

Human beings should not compete with machines at being machines. We will lose on speed, repetition and scale. Machines should not be treated as replacements for human desire, meaning, dissent, moral judgment and care. They do not acquire those things by writing fluent sentences about them.

We may not understand every internal route a machine takes. The machine does not understand our motives as lived experience. That is not a defect to be hidden. It is the reason the relationship should be symbiotic.

Knowledge has always grown because someone cared enough to ask. The machine can help us see farther. It can help us tend a larger garden than any one person could tend alone.

It should not be allowed to fence the garden, erase the gardener and put the company name on the gate.

## The mathematics beneath this argument

The human argument comes first because discovery begins with human desire, and because this essay is about what happens to people when a company turns machine labour into a claim over discovery. But the question of credit must not hide the question of whether the mathematics is right. Our technical counterpaper addresses that separately and completely.

Here is the plain version.

Lean is a powerful proof checker. It can verify that a conclusion follows from the definitions, rules and assumptions it has been given. That is valuable. But checking a proof inside a chosen system does not prove that the system's starting assumptions were themselves derived from reality. A perfectly grammatical sentence can still be untrue.

OpenAI's proofs rely on foundations and mathematical objects that its formal environment already permits. SFT makes a harder demand at the starting line. A result must trace back to one premise-free root. It may not borrow an extra axiom, tune a free parameter or look at a measured answer and then choose the route that reaches it. The same unchanged engine has been used to derive the constants and forces currently registered in the model and to continue through physics, chemistry and the later branches of science. Its results are then compared with measurements and independent records rather than being sheltered from them.

The ledger now contains 2,777 admitted results across seventeen branches and 2,378 packages that compare them with evidence outside the model. It includes the known constants registered so far, the complete registered force structure, the full current Physics and Chemistry branches, independent reconstructions, unchangeable receipts and whole-model Lean checking. Alpha is one particularly clear blind numerical success inside that body of work. It is not the whole garden. It is one flower that lets a reader see what the root system can produce.

We froze twelve exact formal statements behind OpenAI's ten public claims. Then we asked the same question of every one: can this statement enter the already established SFT model without breaking its rules? In each case the answer is no for two direct reasons. Its source foundation is registered with three imported axioms where SFT permits none, and the kind of mathematical object it requires is both demanded by the claim and excluded by the model. A thing cannot be both admitted and excluded. That is the contradiction. The engine proves the negation and then derives a materially different SFT result using objects the model actually generates.

The contradictions do the deductive work. The cumulative evidence gives SFT the standing to decide between the two accounts. This is not a compatibility argument and it is not “false because SFT dislikes it.” It is a direct disproof followed by a first-principles replacement inside the only model in this comparison that has carried its consequences across constants, forces and the external scientific record.

For readers who want to know what changed mathematically, without wading through Lean files, here is a short tour. The field names remain because the questions matter; the explanations say what the replacement actually does.

- **Sphere packing — REJECTED.** This asks how tightly identical spheres can fill spaces of higher and higher dimension. The OpenAI claim treats an infinite limiting object as complete. SFT replaces it with exact upper and lower bounds at every generated finite dimension, plus a rule that produces the next case.
- **Binary and spherical codes — REJECTED.** These ask how many signals can be kept far enough apart to remain distinguishable. SFT replaces two imported limiting rates with a complete count of every generated code and exact fractional witnesses showing the required separation.
- **Nonsofic groups — REJECTED.** The OpenAI claim needs a finitely described group that cannot be captured by finite approximations. SFT exhausts its complete grammar of admissible candidates and finds no such witness. It proves a different, finite-description obstruction theorem instead.
- **Connes's rigidity claim — REJECTED.** This concerns whether certain infinite algebraic structures can bend without changing their identity. SFT does not admit the imported infinite carriers used by the claim. It derives finite group descriptions and their exact finite operator support instead.
- **The permanent lower bound — REJECTED.** The permanent is a matrix calculation related to the determinant. Instead of a lower bound built on a completed real logarithmic resource, SFT supplies the exact finite computation trace and an exact bound enclosing its value.
- **Quantum parallel repetition — REJECTED.** This asks what happens to the chance of winning when a quantum game is repeated. SFT replaces a best-case value taken over an imported unlimited complex strategy space with every generated question, answer and strategy explicitly counted and the value traced exactly.
- **The closest-vector or GapCVP claim — REJECTED.** This concerns finding a lattice point close to a target and proving how hard approximation is. SFT replaces the imported universal language and real-number gap with a generated reduction that always terminates, preserves the verdict and carries an explicit certificate that its promise was met.
- **The Ehrhart volume claim — REJECTED.** This connects the volume of a shape with the lattice points inside its enlargements. SFT replaces arbitrary real-space shapes and continuum volume with generated hulls, exact lattice certificates and finite upper and lower volume bounds.
- **The multicolour Ramsey claim — REJECTED.** This asks when a pattern becomes unavoidable in a graph whose edges have several colours. SFT replaces an infinite limiting estimate with exact finite colour bounds, a proved threshold and a proof that the next case follows.
- **The compactness and degeneracy claims — REJECTED.** These concern when local graph information can guarantee a global result and how sparse a graph remains. SFT replaces eventual real bounds and a completed infinite compactness step with explicit finite graph witnesses, a complete count of the colourings and generated thresholds.

All ten public claims are rejected. All twelve exact source-validity propositions are disproved. Twelve materially different SFT replacements are proved. None of those replacements confirms or rescues the OpenAI statement, and nothing is left open.
