# Materials Science branch roadmap

## Scientific ownership

Materials Science owns organized matter at specimen, microstructure, phase and
property scales.  Physics constants and Chemistry identities are admitted
dependencies rather than Materials-owned derivations.

## Layer One — foundational reconstruction

The foundational Materials paper derives:

1. material, specimen, composition, phase, property and traceability;
2. lattice, unit cell, translation, symmetry and reciprocal support;
3. crystal systems, quasicrystal boundaries and phonon structure;
4. point, line, planar and volume defects;
5. grains, interfaces, diffusion, nucleation and growth;
6. stress, strain, elasticity, plasticity and failure;
7. thermal, electrical, magnetic and optical response;
8. electronic bands, carriers and semiconductor organization;
9. superconducting, superfluid and topological material structure;
10. material classes and composite organization;
11. processing paths, phase selection and degradation; and
12. exact measured property and handoff boundaries.

## Layer Two — complete field reconstruction

The field programme covers:

- crystallography, reciprocal structure, diffraction and disorder;
- defects, microstructure, interfaces and multiscale organization;
- phase equilibria, transitions, transformation kinetics and metastability;
- mechanical behaviour, fracture, fatigue, creep, tribology and rheology;
- thermal transport, heat capacity, expansion and thermoelectric response;
- electronic, dielectric, semiconductor and ionic transport;
- magnetism, spin organization, superconductivity and superfluidity;
- optics, photonics, polarization and electromagnetic materials;
- metals, alloys, ceramics, glasses, polymers and composites;
- porous, soft, colloidal, liquid-crystalline and granular materials;
- biomaterials and biologically derived materials;
- nanomaterials, two-dimensional structures and quantum materials;
- surfaces, coatings, adhesion and interfacial response;
- corrosion, oxidation, wear, radiation damage and ageing;
- processing science, solidification, sintering, heat treatment and additive
  organization without confusing engineering choice with law derivation;
- computational materials, structure-property inference and exact uncertainty;
- materials under extreme pressure, temperature, field and deformation; and
- sustainable material cycles and lifecycle observations at a declared scope.

## Publication sequence

The archived Materials paper remains a historical edition.  The successor
foundational edition requires full owner reconciliation and upstream Chemistry
dependencies.  Later versions expand through the complete field census before a
current-knowledge-complete designation is permitted.

## Handoffs

Materials exports specimen and property laws to Engineering, Biology, Medicine,
Earth Science and Astronomy.  Processing demonstrations cannot select the law.
