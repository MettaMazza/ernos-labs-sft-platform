## Scientific ownership

This branch owns the science of formal and classical computation.  It includes
mathematical programming theory, security, learning and scientific computation,
but not software-project management or platform-specific engineering.

## Layer One — foundational reconstruction

The foundational computational kernel derives:

1. Fold observation, resource, encoding and process laws;
2. state, transition, terminal state, cycle and trace;
3. formal languages and grammars;
4. automata and rewriting systems;
5. recursion, binding, substitution and self-application;
6. abstract machines, the SFT tape machine and exact circuits;
7. computational-model equivalence and universality;
8. recognition, enumeration, reduction, halting and undecidability;
9. incompleteness and relative-computation boundaries;
10. time, space, depth, communication, query and information resources;
11. semantics, types, correctness and verification; and
12. deterministic-support accounts of randomized computation.

The standalone Fold machine demonstrations must keep theorem demonstrations,
bounded exhaustive investigations and frontier conjectures visibly distinct.

## Layer Two — complete field reconstruction

The full field programme is organized into nine owned subbranches.

### Formal computation

- languages, grammars, automata, transducers and rewriting;
- recursive and lambda-like calculi;
- abstract machines, circuits and operational processes;
- composition, decomposition, equivalence and universality.

### Computability

- decidability, recognizability, enumeration and reductions;
- halting, diagonalization and universal machines;
- degrees, relative computation, oracle boundaries and incompleteness;
- explicit admissibility limits for hypercomputation claims.

### Complexity

- input and problem size;
- time, space, circuit size and depth;
- communication, query, descriptive and information complexity;
- randomness, reversibility and parallel resources;
- reductions, completeness and lower/upper bounds;
- average/worst case, approximation and parameterized complexity.

### Algorithms and mathematical data structures

- search, order, arithmetic, strings and sequences;
- trees, graphs, algebraic and geometric algorithms;
- dynamic programming and optimization;
- randomized, parallel, distributed, online and streaming algorithms;
- numerical, symbolic and approximation algorithms.

### Semantics and programming theory

- syntax, binding, substitution and evaluation;
- operational and denotational correspondence;
- type theory, program equivalence and termination;
- correctness, specification, transformation and verification;
- compilation as semantics-preserving translation.

### Concurrent and distributed computation

- causality, synchronization, partial orders and communication;
- consensus, agreement, impossibility, replication and consistency;
- faults, knowledge, locality and network computation.

### Cryptography and computational security

- one-wayness, secrecy, integrity and authentication;
- hashes, commitments, signatures and proofs of knowledge;
- zero knowledge, multiparty and adversarial computation;
- information-theoretic, computational and post-quantum security.

### Learning and intelligence theory

- inference, representation, classification and prediction;
- generalization, sample complexity and optimization;
- induction, planning, reinforcement and multi-agent learning;
- adaptation, interpretability, verification and computational limits.

### Scientific computation

- exact and approximate calculation, stability and convergence;
- discretization, simulation and computational dynamics;
- inverse problems, statistics and high-dimensional computation;
- many-body, symbolic and mathematical modelling methods.
