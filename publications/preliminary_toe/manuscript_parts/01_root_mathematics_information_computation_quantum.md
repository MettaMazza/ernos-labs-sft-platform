## Method and the operational root

### The problem before the first premise

A premise-free programme cannot begin by assuming a conventional logical
system, a number, a set, a space or a physical object. Any such starting point
would already contain the structure the programme claims to derive. The first
question must therefore be narrower: what can be said about an object that has
entered an operational statement, denial, record, proof or derivation?

The registered root theorem is `SFT-ROOT-THERE-IS-NO-NOTHING`:

> No admissible operational statement, denial, record, proof object or
> derivational object is nothing: absence presents no counterexample, while
> every presented counterexample is an occurrence.

Its generator partitions every purported counterexample by one relation:
whether it presents an identifiable object at the proof boundary. The complete
declared grammar has two classes. `unpresented-absence` supplies no statement,
denial, trace or identity and therefore supplies no counterexample. A
`presented-occurrence` supplies an identifiable presentation; that presentation
is itself an occurrence and therefore is not nothing. Of the two generated
classes, exactly one is executable as a purported counterexample, and its
execution defeats the purported counterexample.

The claim has two generated candidates, one unique survivor, four passed
controls and depth-independent closure inside its stated operational boundary.
The controls reject a false premise with no counterexample identity, changed
source custody, a tampered two-survivor artefact and an attempted expansion
into an unexpressed metaphysical domain. An implementation-distinct Python
reconstruction regenerates the partition without importing an SFT module. The
result is a formal operational theorem. It does not claim to describe an
unpresented metaphysical domain; it establishes the root required for a public,
machine-checkable derivation.

### The admission constitution

The two founding results establish more than vocabulary. They demonstrate the
route every later SFT claim must follow. A registered claim records its exact
statement, dependencies, exclusions, provenance, candidate generator,
candidate boundary and intended generality certificate before it can acquire
model authority. The admission sequence is:

1. register the claim and its dependency identities;
2. generate the complete declared candidate grammar;
3. retain one decision for every candidate;
4. require exactly one survivor and prove its minimality and named-shape
   uniqueness;
5. establish finite, finite-successor or depth-independent closure at the
   declared boundary;
6. execute the required false-premise, changed-source, tampered-artifact and
   boundary controls;
7. seal the derivation and source identities;
8. reconstruct the result through an implementation-distinct route;
9. where nature is claimed, register and seal the consequence before target
   access, then preserve the complete observation and comparison record; and
10. issue an unchanged-engine receipt only after every applicable gate passes.

This is a fail-closed route. A successful example, conventional name,
institutional endorsement, benchmark score or attractive application cannot
replace the candidate census. A failed control, missing dependency, changed
source or duplicate survivor halts admission. An application may consume an
admitted law and may later test it, but it cannot tune the earlier grammar or
select the law that the application is then said to support.

The mathematical constitution excludes imported axioms, fitted, trained, free
or target-derived parameters, semantic numerical zero, negative proof
magnitudes, irrational or imaginary proof scalars, floating-point proof
equality, completed infinity and an ungenerated continuum. These exclusions
do not deny that host programs use indices, empty containers, Boolean values
and process return codes. Those objects are quarantined implementation
mechanics: they may count or execute evidence but cannot become SFT proof
objects. Likewise, conventional mathematical and scientific descriptions may
enter later as declared correspondences or measurements; they may not flow
backwards into the derivation.

### The empirical constitution

Formal forcing and empirical testing answer different questions. Candidate
generation asks which structure survives a declared grammar. Empirical testing
asks what a source-bound observation establishes after the formal consequence
has been sealed. For a natural-science claim, capability closure precedes a
value-free target registry; target custody remains separate from the predicting
process; the executable predictor and environment are sealed; target or
comparator output opens only afterwards; and every favourable, adverse, null,
failed, missing and unavailable row remains in the record.

A post-target calculation remains post-target. A development-observed result
remains development-observed. A failed transport is a failed transport rather
than an absence in nature. An unresolved source is not a null result. Later
evidence may lawfully change a current classification when the registered
protocol permits it, but it cannot become an earlier blind prediction by
editorial declaration.

The engine's complete executable-line coverage verifies which implementation
lines the tests executed. It does not prove the absence of every software
defect, complete an omitted candidate grammar or empirically confirm a natural
law. Cryptographic hashes identify the exact artefacts that were checked; they
do not make those artefacts immune from scientific criticism.

### What an admitted claim actually contains

The admission route is easiest to misunderstand when it is reduced to the
phrase “the computer checked it”. The computer does not receive a sentence and
decide whether the sentence sounds true. A claim package is a typed collection
of objects whose identities and relations are fixed before the final decision.
The human-readable statement names the scientific proposition, but model
authority belongs to the registered package rather than to persuasive prose.

The registration names the claim ID, title, owner, dependencies, excluded
inputs, candidate boundary, generation rule, intended certificate and required
controls. Each dependency must already possess a model-admitted receipt, and
the registered route must lead to `SFT-ROOT-THERE-IS-NO-NOTHING`. A narrative
citation to an upstream paper is not enough. The engine resolves the actual
receipt identities, so a missing dependency or a reference to a merely proposed
claim halts the route before enumeration.

Candidate generation then produces the literal declared product. A coordinate
may describe coverage, order, labelling, overlap, source custody, empirical
chronology or any other distinction that the claim says exhausts its boundary.
Every product member receives a stable candidate identity. The decision ledger
must contain one and only one decision for each identity. This requirement
prevents three common forms of retrospective selection: generating only the
favourable alternatives, silently dropping an awkward case, or adding a new
alternative after seeing which structure appears to work.

Elimination is ordered and explicit. A rejected candidate records its first
decisive failure, while the survivor records the complete set of preserving
properties. “Unique” means that the complete registered product contains one
survivor, not that the prose happens to discuss only one preferred object.
“Minimal” means that no less structured candidate within the grammar preserves
the requirements. “Named-shape uniqueness” checks that the surviving identity
and exact form are the ones declared by the theorem rather than an accidental
isomorphic or mislabelled result.

Closure is a separate question. A finite census can establish only its exact
finite boundary unless the package also supplies a lawful successor or
depth-independent certificate. A base-and-successor proof may cover every
supplied positive finite depth without constructing a completed infinite
object. A bounded search remains bounded when no such certificate exists.
This distinction is why a 10,000-value prime census and an unrestricted
positive-finite Fold recurrence can both be exact without having the same
generality class.

The certificate binds the claim, grammar, decisions, survivor, controls,
source manifest, derivation seal, independent implementation and closure
scope. The final receipt is not a free-standing badge. It is an identity map
to those underlying artefacts. If a source changes, the old hash does not
bless the new source. If a candidate is added, the earlier completeness
certificate does not cover it. If a law is extended, the extension receives a
new claim and receipt rather than inheriting authority by similarity.

### Why four controls recur

The repeated four-control structure is methodological rather than decorative.
Each control attacks a different route by which an invalid result could appear
to pass.

The **false-premise control** supplies an object that should fail the scientific
requirement. For the root it attempts to use unpresented absence as a
counterexample; for Fold it proposes the unchanged identity as a non-trivial
distinction; for an error-correction claim it may supply an ambiguous or
exceeding-budget image. A system that accepts both the result and its explicit
false premise has not discriminated the law.

The **changed-source control** alters a registered source or dependency
identity. Its purpose is not to show that cryptography proves the scientific
claim. It shows that a different input cannot silently reuse the old decision
and evidence chain. Source identity is scientifically important wherever
changing a source could change the result, and chronologically important where
the source must remain inaccessible until a seal.

The **tampered-artifact control** changes a candidate set, decision vector,
survivor, certificate or output. It tests whether the implementation notices a
mechanically plausible but unauthorised result. A missing decision, duplicate
survivor or altered terminal value must halt rather than being repaired by a
reader's charitable interpretation.

The **boundary control** tries to import something the claim excludes or to
expand the conclusion beyond its registered domain. Depending on the branch,
that may be a numerical zero, a signed or floating proof value, a stochastic
cause, an external answer table, an application result, a physical measurement
inside a formal theorem, or an unrestricted conventional conclusion from a
native bounded result. Passing the boundary control verifies that the
implementation rejected the prohibited expansion. It does not establish that no
other boundary criticism is possible.

The controls are called adverse because they are designed to make the route
fail in known ways. A passed adverse control means the expected rejection
occurred. That must not be confused with an adverse scientific observation,
which is a registered empirical result conflicting with the tested claim.
Both remain visible, but their evidence classes differ.

### Independent reconstruction and model authority

The main derivation and its validator cannot be the same executable path under
two filenames. An implementation-distinct reconstruction must regenerate the
declared object without importing the scientific module whose result it is
checking. This reduces the risk that one local coding error is merely repeated
by the validator. It does not make the two programs logically independent in
every possible sense; they may still share a mistaken specification. That is
why candidate completeness and specialist criticism remain necessary.

The engine is intentionally narrower than Maria Smith's scientific and
publication authority. It can establish that a registered package passed its
encoded gates under the protected engine and verifier identities. It cannot
decide whether Maria Smith approves publication, whether a conventional term
is rhetorically helpful, whether a newly proposed empirical interpretation
belongs to the claimed owner, or whether an unregistered specialist objection
has scientific force. Conversely, publication does not cause model admission.
An attractively edited or publicly deposited paper cannot turn a halted
package into an accepted claim.

This separation also applies to agent assistance. An agent may generate prose,
run a verifier, compare hashes or identify a discrepancy. It cannot alter
Maria Smith's authorship, approve a release on her behalf, grant a new claim
scientific status or delete an adverse record because it complicates the
narrative. The public repository makes that limitation inspectable by keeping
the machine authority, publication receipt and human authorship records
separate.

### Formal, computational and natural evidence

The same word can obscure very different evidential acts, so this monograph
reserves its verbs. A theorem is **proved** only at its formal grammar and
dependency boundary. A structure is **derived** when its alternatives are
generated and eliminated to the surviving form. An executable program
**implements** or **demonstrates** a derivation; successful execution does not
by itself measure the world. A consequence is a **prediction** only when its
target identity and chronology were correctly sealed before target access. A
source-bound external row is an **observation**, and a value tied to instrument,
method, condition and uncertainty is a **measurement**.

A post-derivation relation between an SFT form and a conventional form is a
**correspondence**. Structural correspondence concerns form; exact numerical
correspondence concerns equality or an exact interval relation; boundary
correspondence is confined to a named interface or limit. Evidence is
**compatible** when it is non-adverse but non-discriminating, and **supporting**
when relevant but non-unique. **Confirmation** is used only where the current
registered evidence protocol grants that classification. A conflicting
registered outcome is **adverse**. Evidence remains **unresolved** when the
required source is unavailable, incomplete, disputed or not yet sufficiently
classified.

These distinctions are not stylistic modesty. They preserve chronology and
falsifiability. A mathematical identity can correspond exactly to an observed
number without having predicted that number. A program can validate its own
trace without validating a physical theory. A later successful transport can
resolve a current access problem without erasing the historical failed
transport. A current adverse row can remain historically visible after a
lawful new claim at a stronger boundary succeeds.

### Threat model and limitations of the platform

The repository architecture addresses identifiable failure modes: source
drift, missing candidates, duplicate or missing decisions, hidden survivors,
changed controls, altered receipts, dependency substitution, post-seal target
access, suppressed rows and silent authority changes. Cross-platform commands
and standard-library implementations reduce environmental opacity. Source
manifests and hashes permit a reproducer to identify the exact object whose
status is being discussed.

It does not eliminate every threat. A registered candidate grammar may omit a
scientifically necessary coordinate that no current test anticipates. Two
implementations may share the same conceptual error. A source may be authentic
but inappropriate for the claim. A measurement protocol may preserve its rows
and still be poorly designed. A hash may bind a mistaken artefact perfectly.
No finite test suite proves the absence of all defects. Domain experts must
therefore inspect the definitions, boundaries, eliminations, controls and
empirical methods rather than treating the receipt as an oracle.

The clean-room rule controls answer flow from V1 and V2. Earlier work may
identify questions to reconstruct and may be compared after the V3 result is
sealed, but earlier certificates, executables and answer tables cannot enter
the new derivation runtime. The reconciliation layer then asks whether the V3
result preserves, corrects, narrows or leaves open each prior owned atomic
obligation. A clean room without reconciliation could omit history; a
reconciliation without a clean room could copy the answer. Both stages are
required.

### Open authority and falsification

The Ernos Labs platform separates authorship, publication authority,

### 8.3 Count, part and exact equality

`SFT-FOUNDATION-COUNT-001` resolves the first question left by One: how can
plural occurrence be represented without assuming arithmetic? A generated
non-empty finite succession retains every generated occurrence exactly once
and adds none. Its ordered generation trace is its positive count. The theorem
does not begin with a numeral; the familiar numeral is a later interface label
for the already generated trace.

A count alone does not identify a portion of a whole. `SFT-FOUNDATION-PART-001`
therefore starts from a complete, disjoint, equal-fibred partition of One and a
non-empty held selection. The exact coordinate is the positive count of held
fibres relative to the positive count of the complete partition. Equality
across different partitions cannot be imported as fraction reduction.
`SFT-FOUNDATION-PART-EQUIVALENCE-001` generates the pair-cell common refinement
of two partitions, lifts both selections completely and requires a one-to-one
and onto pairing of the lifted held cells. The resulting equivalence is
reflexive, symmetric, transitive and stable under uniform refinement without
requiring an external scale.

This establishes the exact positive part carrier used throughout the corpus.
Empty selection remains structural empty One rather than a numerical proof
value. Opposition is retained by a held label identifying which side owns an
unmatched positive remainder. The constitution can therefore represent
absence, order and orientation without importing signed arithmetic at the
foundation.

### 8.4 The minimal Fold

The next unresolved problem is change. Identity leaves One unchanged, while an
arbitrary partition would introduce a selector or scale not present in the
unqualified whole. `SFT-FOUNDATION-FOLD-001` exhausts 288 combinations of whole
coverage, fibre overlap, fibre equality, extension class, label relation,
return relation and extra data. The unique survivor is complete, disjoint and
equal; uses the first positive-count extension; retains two distinct held
labels; includes an exact return to One; and adds no free datum.

That survivor is the minimal **Fold**. Its two labels are structural identities,
not positive and negative numerical quantities. The return relation matters:
without it, the partition is not established as a reversible distinction of
the source whole. The candidate eliminations also establish minimality. The
identity has no non-trivial distinction; every later extension is non-minimal
because the first extension already supplies one; overlapping or unequal
fibres fail exact partition; unheld or identical labels fail operational
distinguishability.

Repeated Fold must then be generated rather than presumed to be binary
notation. `SFT-FOUNDATION-FOLD-ASSEMBLY-001` begins from the structural empty
One word. At each finite successor, it appends each of the two held labels once
to every preceding word, retaining predecessor, transition and return traces.
The first five generated support sizes are conventionally displayed as 2, 4,
8, 16 and 32, but the generality certificate is the base-and-successor
construction, not extrapolation from those examples.

### 8.5 Exact operations, half-One and dynamics

The exact operational interface acts on positive rational parts through One.
Complete turns cast to One rather than to numerical absence. Junction combines
positive traces; product is generated pair-cell support; `Take(a,b)` is
admissible only when `a` is strictly larger than `b`; unison is the exact One
relation. Operational Fold is self-junction followed by complete-One cast:

\[
\operatorname{Fold}(x)=\operatorname{cast}(x\mathbin{\mathrm{joined}}x).
\]

Thus `cast(2)=One`, `Fold(3/4)=1/2`, and
`Take(One,1/4)=3/4` in the exact registered examples. Exact forms precede
their conventional notation; the notation does not select the operation.

The first Fold forces the unique proper one-of-two ground, **half-One**. Either
singleton held fibre self-junctions to One, has an equal-coordinate complement
and Folds to One; One itself is Fold-fixed. Version 1.3 explicitly corrects a
terminology collision retained from earlier work. Complement and phase
translation are different operations:

\[
C(x)=\operatorname{Take}(One,x),
\qquad
A(x)=\operatorname{cast}(x+\text{half-One}).
\]

Half-One is self-complementary, so `C(half-One)=half-One`; its half-One phase
translation casts to One, so `A(half-One)=One`. Both prior structural results
remain preserved, but the names are no longer conflated.

Fold dynamics then follows. For each image `y`, one preimage is `y/2` and the
other is its half-One phase partner. The phase map is involutive and Fold
identifies each phase pair:

\[
\operatorname{Fold}(A(x))=\operatorname{Fold}(x).
\]

For every generated even partition, phase-paired positions have the same Fold
image and the distinct images form the next uniform partition. The first
non-static recurrent orbit is exact:

\[
\frac{1}{3}\longmapsto\frac{2}{3}\longmapsto\frac{1}{3}.
\]

The primitive-map theorem scopes its stronger uniqueness claim carefully. Its
normalised zero-parameter grammar contains identity, square, constant One and
Fold. Base-four enumeration executes 4 one-operation words, 16 two-operation
words and 64 three-operation words, 84 in total. Identity is static; square is
injective and contracts every proper part; constant One collapses; Fold alone
is both non-injective and non-statically recurrent at size one. Positive
construction-size induction prevents any larger composition from displacing
that already qualifying size-one survivor. This is uniqueness within the
registered grammar and generator predicate, not an unrestricted theorem over
every imaginable expression language.
