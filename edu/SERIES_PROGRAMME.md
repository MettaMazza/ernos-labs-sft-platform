## Phase 3: produce progressively

Follow prerequisites: repaired Early Years; ages 5–7 shared foundations; ages 7–11 shared foundations; then the branch ladders from introductory to advanced. The existing syllabus's branch waves are a starting dependency map, not permission to skip readers' prerequisites.

For each book/level pair:

- Write learner outcomes and a concrete story/problem before assets or code.
- Draft the complete book for its own medium; provide demonstrations, varied activities, worked examples, retrieval and explanations appropriate to age.
- Develop an adapted game level with a clear introduction, concrete purpose, distinctive mechanics, recoverable failure, replay variation, relevant guest and a direct lesson ending.
- Reuse stable emojis for repeated teaching items. Use diagrams when precision matters and generated illustrations for appropriate character/setting work.
- Produce a versioned PDF, accessible edition, editable source, useful image descriptions and adult/teacher/answer material. Credit Maria & Matthew Smith throughout.
- Integrate matching Kokoro narration and suitable background music; preserve readability without sound.
- Manually review every page and play every stage, including failure, reset, resume and transitions. Check mobile fit, controls, saved progress, app switching and obsolete audio.
- Complete review QA, push only scoped education files to main, present the deliverables and record pending human review. Continue independent work without treating silence as final approval.

## Completion definitions

A draft has content but is not a finished edition. A review-ready book/level has complete learner content, required companion materials and recorded visual/play checks, with no unresolved release-blocking defect. User-approved means explicit approval of that exact edition. Published-final means that approved edition has been placed in publications/education with its update history.

The whole goal is complete only when the agreed catalogue across every age/stage has delivered books and corresponding game content, required supporting material, actual QA, scoped main releases and resolved final-approval obligations. Track source gaps and approval gaps explicitly. Do not mark the whole goal complete at the end of Phase 1.
