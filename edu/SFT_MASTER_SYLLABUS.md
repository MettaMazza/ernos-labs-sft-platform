# Smithian Fold Theory Open Education Master Syllabus

Planning version: 0.1
Knowledge boundary: repository inspected 30 July 2026
Status authority: 29 July 2026 live programme ledger

## 1. Educational aim

Create a free, coherent library in which a learner can begin with noticing,
sorting, whole-and-part play and honest checking, then progress to exact SFT
derivation, independent reconstruction and research-level criticism across all
sixteen current branches.

The library is SFT-first. It is not a conventional curriculum rewritten with
Fold vocabulary. Every branch begins at its lawful upstream dependencies and
teaches external models only after the SFT account, under an explicit
correspondence or measured-comparison label.

## 2. Programme architecture

The long-term library contains a shared early trunk followed by a complete
branch ladder.

Long-term total: **104 core books**, before workbooks, teacher guides, answer
books, practical manuals and accessibility editions. This is an architecture,
not a promise to release immature branches early.

England's EYFS and key-stage ages, Wales's broad progression steps, Scotland's
curriculum levels and UK qualification practices are navigation references.
They calibrate presentation and progression only. Nation-specific alignment
supplements will be produced separately.

## 4. Early Years shared trunk — four books

These are read-aloud picture/activity books with a short adult guide. They use
safe manipulatives, talk, drawing and movement. They do not pretend that a
metaphor is a formal proof.

### E01 — *Something Is Here*

**SFT source:** premise-free root theorem.
**Units:** seeing and hearing; pointing to what is present; trying to show
“nothing”; the difference between an empty space and no presented thing;
telling what happened.
**Outcome:** the child can distinguish “I see an empty place” from “there is no
presented observation.”

### E02 — *One Whole, Many Parts*

**SFT source:** One, positive finite count, exact positive parts, exact
arithmetic, junction addition and reassembly.
**Units:** one object; equal and unequal visible shares; matching parts;
joining separate one-part counts; reading plus and equals; rebuilding a whole;
no missing piece hidden by language. Fractions remain outside E02.
**Outcome:** the child can make and describe simple whole–part relations, form
`1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 4 parts`, explain that both sides have the same total, and
distinguish that equation from rebuilding four parts as one whole object.

### E03 — *The Fold Makes a Pattern*

**SFT source:** minimal Fold, two held labels, transition and recurrence.
**Units:** two sides or roles; over/under and turn/return activities; repeated
patterns; predicting the next lawful move; spotting a broken rule.
**Outcome:** the child can continue a declared pattern and explain the rule in
ordinary language.

### E04 — *Look Again: How We Check*

**SFT source:** trace, rejection, measurement boundary and independent check.
**Units:** build it; cover it; rebuild it; ask a friend to check; keep the
attempt that failed; observation is not guessing.
**Outcome:** the child understands that checking may confirm, disagree or show
that more work is needed.

## 5. Ages 5–7 shared trunk — eight books

### P1-01 — *There Is No Nothing*

Root story, denial activity, empty form versus numerical count, safe language
for presence and absence.

### P1-02 — *The One and Its Exact Parts*

Positive finite counts, unit fractions with physical models, equivalent parts,
complete reassembly and honest remainder language.

### P1-03 — *Two Labels and the First Fold*

Held distinction, complement, direction, transition, recurrence and pattern
traces.

### P1-04 — *All the Ways We Can Build It*

Small complete candidate sets, sorting allowed and disallowed forms, minimal
survivor and why choosing a favourite is not a proof.

### P1-05 — *Messages, Records and Simple Machines*

Symbols, encoding, source and receiver, state, action, trace, simple automata
and error checking. Introduces Information Science and Computation.

### P1-06 — *Motion, Matter and Change*

Observation, comparison, repetition, movement, waves, material identity and
chemical change at a strictly descriptive foundation. Introduces Physics,
Chemistry and Materials without importing conventional laws as premises.

### P1-07 — *Living, Feeling and Learning Together*

Living boundary, cells as bounded living organization, organism/environment,
interior report, other minds, groups, cooperation and evidence boundaries.
Introduces Biology, Medicine, Consciousness and Social Systems.

### P1-08 — *Earth, Sky and Things We Make*

Earth systems, sky observations, requirements, components, safe tests and the
difference between discovering a relation and building with it. Introduces
Earth, Astronomy, Engineering and cautious synthesis.

## 8. S1 primer syllabus, ages 11–14

Each primer uses six recurring parts:

1. **Objects and distinctions** — what the branch owns.
2. **Operations and relations** — what can lawfully change or compose.
3. **Generated alternatives** — all forms in a manageable declared grammar.
4. **Survivor and boundary** — why one form survives and how far the result
   reaches.
5. **Evidence** — derivation, independent check and measurement kept separate.
6. **Handoffs and open questions** — what belongs upstream, downstream or
   remains unresolved.

End-of-book performance task: construct and present one complete small SFT
investigation with question, dependencies, candidate set, decisions, result,
check, provenance labels and a truthful boundary statement.

## 9. S2 GCSE-age syllabus

### Common core in every branch textbook

- precise definitions and key terminology;
- dependency chains and root traces;
- exact finite arithmetic and lawful notation;
- complete candidate generation at examinable scale;
- minimality, uniqueness and boundary statements;
- provenance classification;
- adverse controls and rejection reasoning;
- measurement, uncertainty and comparison where relevant;
- practical or computational work with risk and trace records;
- structured questions, extended explanations and synoptic problems; and
- complete answer reasoning, not answer-only keys.

### Proposed SFT assessment objectives

| Objective | Weight | What is assessed |
|---|---:|---|
| AO-SFT1: Know and describe | 25% | Exact SFT objects, vocabulary, laws and boundaries. |
| AO-SFT2: Derive and generate | 30% | Dependencies, complete candidate sets, exact construction and calculation. |
| AO-SFT3: Check and evaluate | 25% | Rejections, controls, measurements, uncertainty and independent verification. |
| AO-SFT4: Connect and delimit | 20% | Provenance, handoffs, correspondence, open status and unfamiliar transfer. |

### Qualification honesty

These are GCSE-style educational books, not an approved GCSE specification.
Exam-board content is addressed in optional mapping appendices after the SFT
syllabus is fixed. If a conventional topic lacks an admitted SFT derivation,
the mapping says **correspondence only** or **open**, rather than importing the
answer.

## 10. S3 post-16 syllabus

All sixteen post-16 books add:

- symbolic derivation over general generated finite families;
- depth-independent versus size-bounded closure;
- exact enclosure and correspondence for excluded conventional objects;
- source identity, preregistration, sealing and post-seal measurement;
- family-level dependency and ownership graphs;
- implementation-distinct reconstruction;
- formal essays that compare SFT and conventional accounts without confusing
  correspondence with authority; and
- an extended investigation with reproducible artifacts.

Assessment: two written papers, one exact derivation portfolio, one practical
or computational evidence portfolio and an oral boundary defence.

## 11. Undergraduate introduction syllabus

The undergraduate series is repository-facing. A learner should finish able to
read the scientific modules, claim package and receipts without relying on a
summary.

Common units:

1. constitutional domain and prohibited imports;
2. claim schema and provenance;
3. candidate grammar and census construction;
4. eliminators, minimality and uniqueness;
5. controls and tamper cases;
6. formal versus empirical evidence;
7. independent certification;
8. ownership, handoffs and publication boundaries;
9. branch-specific complete families or admitted foundations; and
10. replication project.

Completion standard: independently reconstruct a selected admitted claim from
declared non-answering inputs, reproduce its result through a distinct route,
and explain exactly what the exercise does and does not establish.

## 12. Advanced study syllabus

Advanced handbooks are living, versioned maps rather than conventional static
textbooks. Each contains:

- the branch's live obligation census;
- admitted family summaries with receipt and certificate maps;
- preserved adverse, null and unavailable results;
- exact open obligations and their prerequisite boundary;
- replication protocols;
- lawful proposal and preregistration templates;
- reviewer checklists;
- historical correspondence after derivation; and
- an update log that never overwrites an earlier scientific boundary.

No advanced educational exercise may perform an official admission, publish,
push, issue a DOI or alter remote scientific state without Maria Smith's
specific authorization.

## 13. Standard chapter design

### Learner-facing sequence

1. **The question** — one precise problem.
2. **What you need** — admitted prerequisites only.
3. **WHY** — meaning and motivation, explicitly not evidence.
4. **DERIVATION** — generated forms, decisions and survivor.
5. **CHECK** — reconstruction, controls and measurement.
6. **What failed and why** — rejected alternatives and adverse outcomes.
7. **Where it stops** — exact boundary and `OPEN` items.
8. **Compare carefully** — external terminology or model only after SFT.
9. **Practice** — retrieval, worked, mixed and unfamiliar tasks.
10. **Build or investigate** — safe application with a trace record.

### Teacher and technical apparatus

- claim IDs and receipt hashes;
- dependency list;
- provenance for each headline statement;
- common misconceptions, including conventional imports;
- differentiation and accessibility options;
- activity safety;
- complete solutions and marking rationales;
- curriculum navigation appendix; and
- changes since the previous edition.

## 14. Exercise progression

Every chapter uses a deliberate ladder:

1. **Remember** — retrieve exact vocabulary and boundaries.
2. **Recognise** — classify examples and non-examples.
3. **Rebuild** — follow a worked derivation with changed labels or size.
4. **Generate** — enumerate the declared complete candidate space.
5. **Decide** — apply eliminators and retain all outcomes.
6. **Check** — use an independent representation or experiment.
7. **Distinguish** — separate derivation, observation, correspondence and use.
8. **Transfer** — solve an unfamiliar but lawfully bounded problem.
9. **Critique** — find a missing premise, target leak or overstated boundary.
10. **Extend carefully** — propose a new question without claiming its answer.

## 16. Status-aware book rules

### Complete-field branches

Mathematics, Information Science, Classical Computation, Reversible and Quantum
Computation, Physics, Chemistry and Materials may receive full current-field
textbooks at every suitable level. “Complete” always means complete to the
dated registered boundary and open to later extension.

### Active Biology branch

Biology books may teach its 82 admitted claims. The remaining 342 obligations
are shown as a labelled field map, research questions and future-volume plan.
They are not silently supplied by external biology.

### Foundation-complete later branches

Medicine, Consciousness, Earth, Astronomy, Social Systems and Engineering may
receive foundation editions based on their admitted claims and bounded return
families. Their comprehensive specialty or full-field books remain outlined
but unpublished as scientific textbooks until their censuses and claims close.

### Synthesis

The twelve admitted synthesis claims may be taught at appropriate advanced
levels. The title, cover and conclusions must never say the final Theory of
Everything is complete while the final assembly gates remain open.

## 17. Production sequence

### Wave 0 — infrastructure and pilot standard

1. approve this charter and master syllabus;
2. choose the open licences for text, code and media;
3. establish accessible page templates and provenance icons;
4. build the claim-to-chapter source manifest format;
5. pilot one early-years book and one rigorous Foundation book; and
6. test with subject, education, accessibility and safeguarding reviewers.

### Wave 1 — the complete Foundation ladder

Produce E01–E04, P1-01–P1-04, P2-01 and the FND books from S1 through A. This
sets the vocabulary and scientific habits every other book needs.

### Wave 2 — exact reasoning and machines

Produce Mathematics, Information Science, Classical Computation and Reversible
and Quantum Computation ladders. These branches are full-field complete and
form the main tool spine for later sciences.

### Wave 3 — physical world

Produce Physics, Chemistry and Materials ladders. Include strong laboratory
safety, measurement custody and standing-prediction labels.

### Wave 4 — admitted life and health foundations

Produce Biology only to its live admitted boundary, followed by Medicine and
Consciousness foundation editions. Expand only after lawful new receipts.

### Wave 5 — planet, cosmos and collective life

Produce Earth, Astronomy and Social foundation editions with strict
history/context/measurement boundaries.

### Wave 6 — translation and synthesis

Produce Engineering foundation and protocol texts, then the twelve-claim
Synthesis reader. The final ToE educational capstone waits for the scientific
assembly gate.

## 20. Definition of educational completion

A book is educationally complete when its teaching artifacts, answers,
accessibility, safety, provenance and age-level reviews pass. That does not
make its scientific branch complete. Scientific status remains exactly what
the immutable receipts and live census say.

The library as a whole is never permanently finished. It remains open to
lawful scientific extensions, educational correction, new accessibility needs
and additional nation-specific curriculum navigation.
