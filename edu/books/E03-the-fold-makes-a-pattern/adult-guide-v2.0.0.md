## What this book teaches

Mia, Sol and Tavi follow the Moon Lantern’s moon–sun light into the garden. The trail stops before the Sunrise Arch. Vee, the book’s only new guest, helps the child inspect one two-sided tile, make one agreed turn, see the other side, turn again, and see the first side return.

The child then uses that visible rule to copy and continue a short recurrence, retain a failed attempt, find the first broken move, repair it, and use the same two-role order with over/under and star/leaf pictures.

The story is complete without the companion game. The game may adapt the same journey with movement and replay, but it does not explain or authorise the book.

## How to share the book

Read slowly enough for the child to inspect every emoji card. On activity pages, pause before turning the page. Accept pointing, gaze, signing, speech, movement or an adult carrying out the child’s direction.

Useful prompts include:

- “What can you see now?”
- “What did one turn change?”
- “Which picture came back?”
- “Where is the first place the order changes?”
- “Does this path reach the arch?”

Do not disclose an answer before the child attempts it. Keep a different first answer visible long enough to compare. Say what changed without calling the child wrong.

## Visible cause chain

1. The rebuilt Moon Lantern shows moon, sun, moon.
2. The light runs into the garden and stops at a dark stone.
3. Vee asks the team to find the trail rule and mend the path.
4. One tile shows a moon side and a sun side.
5. One agreed turn changes moon to sun.
6. The next agreed turn returns moon.
7. Repeating the move makes a moon–sun pattern.
8. Sol’s first try creates the first visible break.
9. The child finds and repairs that one move.
10. The same two-role order crosses the bridge and reaches the Sunrise Arch.

## Activity answers and teaching prompts

### 1. What is showing? — page 7

Answer: moon. The page asks only what is visible now. The sun remains underneath on the same tile.

Say: “Moon is showing because we can see it now.”

### 2. Turn once — page 10

Answer: sun. The child begins with moon showing and carries out one complete turn.

Say: “One agreed turn changed which side we could see.”

### 3. Turn once more — page 12

Answer: moon. The second turn brings the earlier side back.

Say: “Moon returned. Return means an earlier side is showing again.”

### 4. Follow and continue the lights — pages 15–16

The copied row is moon, sun, moon, sun. The next picture on page 16 is moon.

Say: “The same order started again. That repeating order is the pattern.”

### 5. Find the first break — page 19

Answer: card four. The row is moon, sun, moon, moon. After the third moon, the rule requires sun.

Say: “We checked from the start and kept Sol’s first try so we could compare it.”

### 6. Which way comes next? — page 23

Answer: under. The shown route is over, under, over, so the next role is under.

Say: “The names changed, but the take-turns rule stayed the same.”

### 7. Choose the complete path — page 26

Answer: C. Path A stops early. Path B reaches the arch but repeats sun. Path C reaches the arch and keeps moon and sun taking turns.

Say: “We checked the whole path, not only its first step.”

### 8. Remember and transfer — pages 28 and 30

The first gate changes moon to sun. The star–leaf row continues with leaf.

Say: “You remembered an earlier move and used the same rule with new pictures.”

## Why repeated items are emoji

Moon, sun, star, leaf, blank place and Moon Lantern use stable OpenMoji 16.0 assets under CC BY-SA 4.0. Reusing one versioned picture for every repeated item keeps counting, matching and ordering clear. Generated illustration supplies settings, characters and one-off story action only.

## Safe optional play away from the page

The book needs no materials. If useful, an adult may draw a large moon on one side of a card and a large sun on the other. The child may point or direct the adult instead of handling it.

Avoid small loose parts, thrown cards or movement near steps. A hand turn is optional. Never require colour recognition alone; always use the picture and spoken name.

## Accessibility

- Moon, sun, star and leaf differ by picture and name, not colour alone.
- Every activity accepts pointing, gaze, signing, speech or adult-mediated action.
- The answer remains on a later page so thinking time is unlimited.

This is a complete picture book. It closes the Sunrise Arch problem and explains every lesson on its own pages. A child may begin here using the short recap, while the lantern and next-book question preserve series continuity.
