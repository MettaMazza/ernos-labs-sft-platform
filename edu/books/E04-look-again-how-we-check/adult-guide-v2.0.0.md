## What this book teaches

Mia, Sol and Tavi arrive at the garden gate directly from E03. One visible problem drives the whole story: they must rebuild and check a four-picture welcome sign before the gate can open. Ivo is the book’s only new guest.

The child looks before answering, uses a visible plan, keeps a try that does not match, follows four retained steps, repairs the first changed move, and answers width and height as two separate questions. Ivo then makes a fresh sign from the same plan without seeing the team’s sign.

The book is complete without its companion game. The game may adapt the same journey with movement and replay, but it does not explain or authorise the book.

## How to share the book

Read slowly enough for the child to inspect every emoji card. On activity pages, pause before turning the page. Accept pointing, gaze, signing, speech, movement or an adult carrying out the child’s direction.

Useful prompts include:

- “What can you see now?”
- “Which corner are we checking?”
- “Where is the first change?”
- “Are we measuring across or upwards?”
- “Which picture or card answers that question?”

Do not reveal an answer before the child attempts it. Keep a different first answer visible long enough to compare. Say what changed without calling the child wrong.

## Visible cause chain

1. The repaired moon-and-sun trail reaches a closed garden gate.
2. The gate needs one welcome sign that matches a four-picture plan.
3. Ivo joins and invites the child to look again when something needs checking.
4. The child observes the plan and identifies the bee beside the moon.
5. One complete sign is chosen while the plan remains visible.
6. The plan is covered; memory is checked by looking again.
7. Sol’s first try stays visible while two swapped pictures are found.
8. Four step cards reveal the first changed placement.
9. The sign is repaired and measured across, then upwards; Ivo makes a separate sign without seeing the team’s answer; four clear records answer four questions before the gate opens.

## Activity answers and teaching prompts

### 1. Find the moon corner — page 7

Answer: **bee**. Say: “You looked at the plan and answered from what you could see. That is an observation.”

### 2. Which sign matches? — page 10

Answer: **B**. Check all four positions: sunflower with sun, bee with moon, apple with leaf and boot with star.

### 3. What was beside the star? — page 13

Answer: **boot**. “I’m not sure” is acceptable before the plan is uncovered. Looking again checks the memory.

### 4. Spot the two changes — page 15

Answer: **bee and boot**. Sunflower and apple stayed in place. Keep Sol’s first try visible.

### 5. Where did the change begin? — page 17

Answer: **Step 3**. Steps 1 and 2 follow the plan. Step 3 first puts the boot beside the moon.

### 6. Repair the moon corner — page 19

Answer: **bee**. The boot then returns to the star corner.

### 7. How wide is the sign? — page 21

Answer: **4 equal yellow tiles** from left to right. This answers width only.

### 8. How tall is the sign? — page 23

Answer: **3 equal blue tiles** from bottom to top. This is a separate height question.

### 9. Same or different? — page 26

Answer: **same**. Ivo used the same plan but did not see or copy the team sign.

### 10. Choose the picture check — page 28

Answer: **plan and sign**. The width and height tile cards answer different questions.

### 11. Is every check ready? — page 30

Answer: **yes**. Pictures, width, height and Ivo’s own sign each have a visible record.

The eleven paper activities each ask for one short decision. None needs cutting, loose pieces, colour recognition alone or a companion screen.

## Why repeated items are emoji

Sun, moon, leaf, star, sunflower, bee, apple, boot, measuring squares and answer marks use stable OpenMoji 16.0 assets under CC BY-SA 4.0. Reusing one versioned picture for every repeated identity keeps matching, counting and ordering clear. Generated illustration supplies settings, characters and one-off story action only.

## Accessibility

- Every repeated item differs by picture and spoken name, not colour alone.
- Every activity accepts pointing, gaze, signing, speech or adult-mediated action.
- Words appear above their illustrations whenever a picture is labelled.
- Challenge pages leave the answer unmarked; later pages keep the same objects visible and explain the answer.
- Equal measuring tiles have both stable shape and counted position.
- The accessible HTML edition exposes page order, activity status and picture descriptions.
- The PDF is not described as fully tagged; use the semantic HTML for structured access.

## Safe optional play away from the page

The book needs no materials. If useful, an adult may draw a large four-space sign and let the child direct where four large household picture cards should go. Avoid small loose parts. Never require colour recognition alone.

For measuring play, use equal large blocks or hand spans only after naming one direction. Count across for width, then start again and count upwards for height. Do not mix units within one answer.

## Medium independence and continuity

This is a complete picture book. It explains its own mission, every activity and the final lesson. E03 continuity appears in a short recap; a child may begin here without playing a game. The distant Star Door preserves series continuity without naming or teaching an unbuilt next book.
