# E02 narrative and learning design — review 2.0.0

## Book identity

- Title: *One Whole, Many Parts*
- Adventure title: *The Moon Lantern Workshop*
- Audience: ages 3–5
- Permanent team: Mia, Sol and Tavi
- Book guest: Pax, the patient piece-keeper
- Medium: 32-page paper-native picture book that works without the game

## Connected beginning

E01 ends after Mia files the five-star map and a parcel reaches the library rug
by the tower slide. E02 begins with that parcel still on the rug. A child who
starts here is told in one sentence that Mia, Sol and Tavi solved their first
mystery and that this parcel is their next adventure.

The parcel contains one complete Moon Lantern and a picture asking the team to
carry it to the night balcony. The whole lantern is visibly too wide for a
small workshop door. This single physical problem drives every page that
follows.

## Emotional and causal arc

1. The trio recognise the parcel from the end of E01 and open it together.
2. They admire the complete Moon Lantern before it changes.
3. Sol tries the small doorway; the whole lantern does not fit.
4. Pax hears the bump, introduces himself and offers to keep every piece safe.
5. The child first identifies the exact whole that must be rebuilt.
6. Pax opens four safe seams. The same lantern becomes four visible parts.
7. The child counts every part once and carries each through the doorway.
8. The child checks that the parts are the same size and fills one missing place.
9. Mia holds two parts and Pax holds two. The child joins the groups as four parts.
10. All four parts return to their matching places and make the same whole again.
11. The lantern reaches the balcony; Pax says goodbye after the problem is solved.
12. Moon, sun, moon pictures begin taking turns and naturally tease E03.

No room, term, guest, object or reward appears without a visible cause.

## Experience before vocabulary

- **whole** follows the child's comparison of complete, missing and extra
  lantern pictures;
- **part** follows the visible opening of the same lantern along four seams;
- **count** follows a finger route that visits each visible part once;
- **same size** follows a direct visual comparison of pairs;
- **plus** follows two visible groups being considered together;
- **equals** follows a count of the same four visible parts on both sides; and
- **rebuild** follows the placement of every part back into its matching place.

The child book does not use *fibre*, *candidate grammar*, *parameter-free*,
*multiplicity*, *junction trace* or *depth-independent*. Exact source and scope
remain in the adult guide.

## Stable emoji-object vocabulary

The Moon Lantern is always the same OpenMoji 16.0 lantern asset. Its four
puzzle pieces are transparent quarter-clips derived mechanically from that same
emoji. They are not newly generated illustrations. Repeated objects also keep
the series parcel, door, moon, sun and star emoji assets.

Generated illustrations show only the tower, workshop, balcony, character
expressions and story action. The generated art never invents a second lantern
design or substitutes a vague glowing orb.

## Eight paper-native activities

1. **Lantern Detective** — point to the complete lantern, not the missing or
   extra version.
2. **Seam Hunt** — trace the vertical and horizontal seams across the same
   lantern and predict how many parts will open.
3. **Count Keeper** — follow one gold route through the four separated parts,
   saying one number at each part.
4. **Doorway Parade** — trace four separate routes, one for each part, through
   the small door.
5. **Same-Size Pairs** — point to the pair whose two pieces have the same frame.
6. **Missing Place** — choose the one quarter that completes a three-part frame
   with no gap or overlap.
7. **Add Together** — count two parts with Mia and two with Pax, then choose the
   total from 3, 4 or 5.
8. **Lantern Builder** — trace each quarter to its matching place in the frame;
   the next page reveals the rebuilt whole.

Every answer is on the following page or appears only after the child has made
a genuine attempt. No activity pretends that printed pieces can be digitally
dragged, tapped, rotated or submitted.

## Exact mathematical wording

- `1 part + 1 part + 1 part + 1 part = 4 parts`
- `2 parts + 2 parts = 4 parts`
- `4 parts make 1 whole lantern`

The book never writes `4 = 1`. “Parts” and “whole lantern” name different
things. Fractions, numerical zero and cross-partition equivalence are outside
this edition.

## Direct closing explanation

The narrator speaks to the child:

> You began with one whole lantern. You found its four parts, counted every
> part once, carried all four, and fitted them back together. Plus joined the
> groups. Equals showed the same four parts were counted on both sides. This
> helps us keep every piece and rebuild the whole correctly.

The final wording may be split into shorter child sentences, but it must retain
what was learned, how it was learned and why it matters.
