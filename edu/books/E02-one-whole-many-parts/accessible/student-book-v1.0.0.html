Page 3: THE NEXT ADVENTURE

Last time, Mia, Sol and Tavi looked for nothing. Every clue still gave them something to see, hear or remember.

Then a new parcel slid down the library ramp. Its label asked:

HOW CAN ONE WHOLE HAVE MANY PARTS?

They had learned to look carefully before answering. Now you will help them count each part, carry every part and rebuild the lantern.

Page 4: THE MOON LANTERN

Mia lifted the lid. Inside was a round Moon Lantern and a picture card.

“Please bring the lantern to the balcony before the first evening star,” Mia read.

The card showed the balcony, so everyone knew where to take the lantern.

Page 5: IT WILL NOT FIT

The friends reached a door much smaller than the lantern. Sol tried to carry it through. BUMP! It would not fit.

A purple maker hurried over. “I’m Pax. I made this lantern. Let’s solve this together.”

Their problem is clear: How can the whole lantern get through the small door?

Page 6: PAX HAS A PLAN

Pax rolled over a tray with four spaces. “The lantern opens into four parts,” Pax explained. “We can check the whole, count the parts, carry every part through, and fit them together again.”

Mia smiled. “Then let’s begin with the whole lantern.”

The journey has four steps: check it, count it, carry it and rebuild it.

Page 7: TRY IT · FIND THE COMPLETE LANTERN

Look at the three pictures.

One lantern has an empty space. One has every lantern part. One has an extra triangle.

Which picture matches the lantern from the parcel?

Choose before you turn the page. Use only what you can see in the pictures.

Page 8: ONE WHOLE LANTERN

THIS ONE MATCHES.

Every part is here. There is no empty space and no extra piece.

“When all the parts that belong are here together, we call it one whole,” said Pax.

The whole is the complete Moon Lantern: nothing that belongs is missing, and nothing extra has been added. Find the optional code.

Optional picture code: WHOLELIGHT

Page 9: FOUR CARRYING SPOTS

Pax made four carrying spots glow on the floor.

“Each time one lantern part goes through the door, one spot will light,” Pax said. “If all four spots light, we will know that all four parts made it.”

The glowing spots will help the friends keep track of every lantern part.

Page 10: TRY IT · COUNT EVERY SPOT

Point to each glowing spot in order.

Say one number for each spot: one, two, three, four. Touch every spot once.

This practice will help the friends carry one lantern part for each spot.

Page 11: EVERY SPOT, ONCE

ONE · TWO · THREE · FOUR.

You gave one number to each spot. You did not skip a spot or count one twice.

That is how we count all four exactly.

When the lantern opens, the same careful count will show whether every part is with us.

Page 12: SOL’S BOUNCY COUNT

Sol bounced from spot to spot. “One, two, three, three, four!”

There are four spots, but Sol said five numbers. Something went wrong. Look at the footprints. What happened?

Find the spot that Sol visited more than once before you turn the page.

Page 13: SOL FINDS THE REPEAT

SPOT THREE HAS TWO FOOTPRINTS.

Sol touched that spot twice, so the count was not exact. He started again and touched each spot once: one, two, three, four.

“The footprints helped us find and fix the mistake,” said Tavi.

An exact count includes every spot once: none missed and none repeated. Find the optional code.

Optional picture code: ONCEAROUND

Page 14: CHOOSE THE FOUR-PART PLAN

Pax showed three picture plans for opening the lantern into four parts.

“Only one plan makes four parts the same size, with no empty space and no part covering another,” Pax said.

The right plan must use every bit of the circle, because every bit belongs to the whole lantern.

Page 15: TRY IT · FILL THE CIRCLE

Look at the three round plans.

Which plan makes four parts the same size and fills the whole circle?

Check for an empty space. Check whether one part covers another.

Point to one plan before you turn the page.

Page 16: LOOK FOR MATCHING SIDES

Start at the middle and follow each line to the edge.

Does the left side match the right side? Does the top match the bottom?

Which plan looks balanced around the middle?

Use what you can see. The name for this matching shape comes after your choice.

Page 17: THE SYMMETRICAL PLAN

THIS PLAN FITS.

Four same-size parts fill the circle. No gap. No overlap. Left matches right; top matches bottom.

“Matching sides around the middle make a symmetrical plan,” said Pax.

Together, the four exact parts fill one whole circle. Find the optional code.

Optional picture code: FAIRFIT

Page 18: THE LANTERN OPENS

Pax pressed the centre button. CLICK, CLICK, CLICK, CLICK! The lantern gently opened into four parts.

The four same-size parts rested in four spaces on the tray.

Now the friends can see and count every lantern part before anyone moves it.

Page 19: TRY IT · CARRY EVERY PART

Help the friends carry the four lantern parts through the small door.

Move one part, then light one carrying spot. Use each spot once. Count aloud: one, two, three, four.

Every part must reach the other side. Leave no part behind and move no part twice.

Page 20: ALL FOUR MADE IT THROUGH

ONE · TWO · THREE · FOUR.

Four parts went through the door. Four parts are waiting on the other side. No part is still behind.

The parts moved, but there were still four of them. Find the optional code.

Optional picture code: PARTPAIR

Page 21: CHECK THE SIZE PLAN

Before the rebuild, Pax opened the lantern plan. It showed that every part should be the same size.

Pax placed two picture cards on the table. Which card shows two same-size pieces: Pair A or Pair B?

These are checking pictures, not extra lantern pieces. Look at both pairs before you turn.

Page 22: EQUAL IN SIZE

PAIR A SHOWS EQUAL SIZES.

PAIR B DOES NOT.

The two circles in Pair A are the same size. We say they are equal in size.

Equal can mean the same. Here, we are checking whether the circles are the same size.

Page 23: SPOT THE MISSING PART

To practise spotting a missing part, Pax put three lantern parts into the round frame. The fourth part stayed on the tray beside it.

“Is the whole lantern ready yet?” asked Sol.

Look at the empty space and the part on the tray. Decide before you turn.

Page 24: ONE PART IS MISSING

NOT YET. ONE SPACE IS EMPTY.

Mia moved the fourth part from the tray into the empty space. Now all four spaces were filled.

“We found it because every part stayed in sight,” Mia said.

After the practice check, Pax returned all four parts to the tray for the real rebuild.

Page 25: KEEP THE EXTRA OUTSIDE

Just then, a loose triangle rolled from the worktable and stopped beside the tray.

“We already have all four lantern parts,” said Tavi. “That triangle belongs to the workshop, not the lantern.”

Extra means it does not belong in this whole. Keep it outside the lantern tray. Find the optional code.

Optional picture code: GAPCHECK

Page 26: TWO HERE AND TWO THERE

At the balcony, Pax lifted two lantern parts. Two more stayed on the tray.

Point to the two in Pax’s hands. Now point to the two on the tray.

How many parts can you see altogether?

Count each visible part once before you turn. No lantern part is hidden.

Page 27: TWO PLUS TWO MAKES FOUR

2 IN PAX’S HANDS + 2 ON THE TRAY = 4 PARTS ALTOGETHER.

The plus sign joins the two groups. The equals sign says the count on each side is the same: four parts.

Two parts are held, but all four parts still make the whole lantern. Find the optional code.

Optional picture code: HELDWHOLE

Page 28: COUNT FOUR ONE-PART GROUPS

Next, Pax placed one lantern part in each of four counting spaces.

1 PART + 1 PART + 1 PART + 1 PART = ?

Touch each part once. Is the total 3, 4 or 5 parts?

The plus signs ask us to join the four one-part groups into one count.

Page 29: FOUR PARTS ALTOGETHER

1 PART + 1 PART + 1 PART + 1 PART = 4 PARTS.

Each plus sign joins another one-part group. The equals sign says the count on each side is the same: four parts.

No part was missed, counted twice or added. Both sides count the same four parts.

Page 30: BUILD THE WHOLE AGAIN

Pax placed the four counted parts into the round frame. CLICK, CLICK, CLICK, CLICK!

The four separate parts fitted together. The Moon Lantern was one whole again.

4 PARTS → 1 WHOLE LANTERN. The arrow shows what happened next: the parts were rebuilt. It is not an equals sign.

Page 31: WHAT YOU DISCOVERED

You helped Mia, Sol, Tavi and Pax solve the problem!

A whole has every part that belongs to it. We counted each visible part once. We used plus to join the counts. We used the equals sign to show that the count was the same on both sides. Then those four parts rebuilt one whole lantern.

When you build something from pieces, count and check every part so none is lost, repeated or added.

Page 32: THE FIRST STAR APPEARS

The Moon Lantern lit up as the first evening star appeared. A blue moon glowed across its round face. Then came a gold sun. Then the blue moon returned.

“The pictures are taking turns,” said Tavi. “I wonder what comes next?”
