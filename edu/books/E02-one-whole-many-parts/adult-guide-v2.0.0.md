## What this book teaches

Mia, Sol and Tavi open the parcel that arrived at the end of Book One. It contains one complete Moon Lantern and a simple request: bring it to the balcony. The way is blocked by a little door. The whole lantern is too wide, so the team must open it into four agreed parts, keep every part, carry each one through, and rebuild the same whole.

The child learns through that one visible problem. They first see the complete lantern. They then find its seams, count its four parts once each, compare sizes, identify a missing place, join two groups of two, and rebuild the whole. The words *whole*, *part*, *plus* and *equals* follow the experience instead of replacing it.

Pax is the only new guest in this book. Pax keeps pieces safe and asks questions, but does not supply an answer before the child has tried. Mia, Sol and Tavi remain the permanent team.

## How to share the book

Read the story pages at an unhurried pace. On an activity page, read the short instruction, pause, and let the child point, trace, speak, sign, look or use a communication aid. Do not turn the page immediately. The next page or story beat checks the answer.

Helpful prompts include:

- “Show me what you notice.”
- “Have we touched this part already?”
- “Is any place still empty?”
- “Are these two picture cards the same size?”
- “What are we counting?”

Avoid prompts that disclose the answer, such as “Choose four” or “Put that piece in the bottom corner.” A different first answer is useful evidence. Keep it visible long enough to compare.

## The visible cause chain

1. The parcel contains one complete Moon Lantern.
2. The whole lantern is too wide for the little door.
3. Pax shows two safe seams.
4. The seams open the lantern into four parts.
5. The child counts every part once.
6. Each part travels through the door.
7. The child checks size and a missing place.
8. Two parts plus two parts make four parts.
9. All four parts return to their matching places.
10. The same whole lantern shines on the balcony.

## Activity answers and teaching prompts

### 1. Lantern Detective — page 8

Answer: the middle picture, labelled **SAME WHOLE**. It shows the same complete lantern. The first picture has a missing place. The third has an unrelated star added.

Say: “The whole means all of this lantern, with none of it missing and nothing extra added.”

### 2. Seam Hunt — page 11

The child traces one line down and one line across. The lines divide the lantern picture into four visible parts. Accept a guess before the reveal.

Say: “The seams show where the lantern can open. Now we can check how many parts appear.”

### 3. Count Keeper — page 14

The child follows the gold trail and touches each of the four quarter cards once: one, two, three, four. The important action is not merely reciting numbers; it is keeping one count for each visible part without skipping or repeating one.

Say: “You gave one number to each part. That made the count clear.”

### 4. Doorway Parade — page 17

The child traces four coloured paths through the open door. Every lantern-part card must have a route. The following page shows all four cards together on Pax’s tray.

Say: “Every part came through. No part stayed behind, and no new part was added.”

### 5. Same-Size Pairs — page 20

Answer: Pair A. Its two picture cards have matching outer sizes. Pair B contains one large and one small card. The picture compares visible size only; it does not claim that every object called a part must always be equal.

Say: “Pair A matches in size. Matching sizes help the parts return to matching places.”

### 6. The Missing Place — page 22

Answer: the first choice, the bottom-right lantern part. It completes the frame. The other lantern part belongs in a different position, and the star is not part of this lantern.

Say: “The last agreed part fills the empty place. There is no gap and no overlap.”

### 7. Add Together — page 25

Answer: 4. Mia carries two visible parts and Pax carries two visible parts. Read the next page aloud as “two parts plus two parts equals four parts.”

Say: “Plus joins the two groups. Equals tells us both sides count the same four visible parts.”

### 8. Lantern Builder — page 28

The child traces each quarter card to its matching place in the two-by-two frame. Each part is used once. The next page shows the same whole lantern rebuilt.

Say: “All four parts are back in their places. Four parts make one whole lantern.” Do not shorten this to “four equals one,” because *parts* and *whole lantern* name different things.

## Why the repeated objects are emoji

The Moon Lantern, moon, sun, door, parcel and lantern quarters use stable, versioned OpenMoji assets. The four quarters are mechanical crops of the same OpenMoji lantern, not newly generated pictures. This keeps repeated items clear and recognisable from page to page. Generated illustrations supply setting, character, emotion and one-off story action only.

## Safe optional play away from the page

The book is complete without materials. If useful, an adult may prepare four large card pieces and a large matching frame. Avoid small loose parts, glass lanterns, hot lights and cutting tools within the child’s reach. The child may watch, point or direct an adult rather than handle pieces.

Try these short extensions:

- Put four large cards in a row and touch each once while counting.
- Hide one card and ask which visible place is empty.
- Put two cards on one mat and two on another, then count them together.
- Rebuild the same four-place frame and check for a gap or overlap.

Keep the language tied to what is visible. Say “This place has no card in it” before using a more general word such as *empty*.

## Accessibility

- Accept pointing, gaze, signing, speech, movement and communication aids.
- Describe each picture aloud when helpful.
- Use shape, position, outline, size and words; never colour alone.
- Offer enlarged pages or the semantic HTML edition for scalable text.
- Use tactile outlines or large pieces with different edge textures if helpful.
- Give unlimited thinking time and repeat an activity without penalty.
- Do not describe the PDF as fully tagged; use the semantic HTML for structured access.

## Medium independence and continuity

This is a complete picture book. It does not require the companion game to explain its story, activities, answers or lesson. The game may adapt the same sequence with movement and replay, but it is not evidence for the book and cannot change the SFT boundary.

The ending closes the Moon Lantern problem. The moon–sun–moon pictures offer a gentle question for Book Three without teaching its answer. A child may stop here satisfied or continue in series order.
