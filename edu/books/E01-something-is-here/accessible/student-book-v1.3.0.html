THE NOTHING HUNT

Page 3

A parcel arrived at Mira's Clue Workshop. Inside were a map and a note: FIND NOTHING TO OPEN THE STAR DOOR.

Pip chirped, 'A real mystery!'

HOW TO PLAY

Page 4

The map had five empty star spaces. Each checked clue would light one star. Five stars would open the mystery doors.

A clue is something you notice that helps you look again. First look, then say, then turn to check.

WARM-UP - SPOT 5

Page 5

Mira is in her room. Can you spot five things?

Look carefully. The next page will name the objects.

PRACTICE SEARCH - CHECK

Page 6

Mira checked the room with Pip. Each label named one thing they could see. The map was ready for Clue One.

This was their practice search. No star had lit yet. A tiny code is hidden on this page.

CLUE 1 - THE BOX

Page 7

Mira opened the box. No toy was inside. Did she find nothing?

Look closely before you decide.

CLUE 1 - REVEAL

Page 8

The toy was outside the box. The box was still there. Mira looked inside.

The box was empty, but Mira could still see it.

MAP - 1 OF 5 STARS LIT

Page 9

A container holds things. This box is a container. Empty means the toy is not inside. Clue One checked: the first star lit.

The empty box is still here. Can you find the tiny hidden code?

CLUE 2 - THE BELL

Page 10

The bell did not ring. What did Mira and Pip do while they waited?

Cup your hand by your ear and listen with Mira.

MAP - 2 OF 5 STARS LIT

Page 11

Mira listened. Pip waited. The bell stayed still and quiet. Clue Two checked: the second star lit.

The bell did not ring, but Mira and Pip were still there. Can you find the tiny hidden code?

CLUE 3 - THE BLANK CARD

Page 12

Pip found a card with no drawing on it. 'Nothing!' he chirped. What can you spot?

There are four clues before you turn the page.

MAP - 3 OF 5 STARS LIT

Page 13

Mira could see the card, the pencil, Pip's feather and her magnifying glass. Clue Three checked: the third star lit.

Blank means there is no mark on the card. Can you find the tiny hidden code?

CLUE 4 - HIDDEN WORD

Page 14

Pip arranged seven letter blocks. Can you find the word NOTHING?

Look across the blocks from left to right.

MAP - 4 OF 5 STARS LIT

Page 15

The letters spell NOTHING. We can see and read the word. Clue Four checked: the fourth star lit.

The word is here, even though it names nothing.

CLUE 5 - BEHIND THE CURTAIN

Page 16

Mira put the toys behind a curtain. Part of each toy was hidden. Did the room become nothing?

Spot what stayed in the picture.

MAP COMPLETE - 5 STARS LIT

Page 17

The curtain hid part of each toy. Mira could still see the curtain, part of the toys and Pip. Clue Five checked: the fifth star lit.

A view is what we can see from one place. The map was complete. Can you find the tiny hidden code?

THE STAR DOOR OPENS

Page 18

All five stars shone on the map. The star door unlocked. Behind it stood two mystery doors.

'Now we can test the note,' said Mira.

THE TWO-DOOR GAME

Page 19

Door A holds out a card. Door B does not. Which door gives Mira something to look at?

Something shown for us to look at is called an example.

DOOR A - CHECK

Page 20

Door A shows Mira the card. Mira can look at it and point to it. This card is an example.

An example is something shown for us to look at or check.

DOOR B - CHECK

Page 21

Door B did not show Mira a card. There was no new object from Door B for Mira to look at.

This is what 'no example was given' means. We do not make up a hidden one.

TRY TO BEAT THE GAME

Page 22

Can you draw nothing on this card? You may make a mark or leave the card blank.

Choose first. Then turn the page to check both choices.

CHECK BOTH CHOICES

Page 23

If you drew a mark, the mark is here. If you left it blank, the card is here. Neither choice made nothing appear.

Mira can still point to the mark or the card.

MIRA'S DETECTIVE RULE

Page 24

When someone shows us an object, we have something to look at. When no object is shown, we cannot point to an object from that door.

That is what 'no example was given' means.

TREASURE FOUND

Page 25

There is no nothing.

The parcel's note had led them through every check. Mira and Pip had found the starting clue of Smithian Fold Theory. Can you find the final tiny hidden code?

THE FAIR-PLAY RULE

Page 26

In this game, Mira checks what people show, say, draw or save in a note. She does not guess about things no one has shown.

A careful detective says what the game can check and where the check stops.

FINAL SPOT-THE-CLUE

Page 27

Find four clues: an empty box, a still bell, a blank card and a partly hidden toy.

Name each object first. Then say what is empty, still, blank or hidden.

FINAL SPOT - ANSWERS

Page 28

Box here - no toy inside. Bell here - no ring. Card here - no mark. Curtain here - toy partly hidden.

Mira named what she could see and what was missing.

SORT THE CARDS

Page 29

Which pictures show an object you can look at? Which empty frame means no object was shown yet?

Move your finger from each picture or frame to a basket.

SORT - ANSWERS

Page 30

Box, bell and card: objects to look at. Empty frame: no object was shown yet.

That is what NO EXAMPLE GIVEN means.

NOTHING-HUNT DETECTIVE

Page 31

I spotted. I wondered. I checked. I kept the surprises.

Add your name, mark or sticker with a grown-up.

LEVEL COMPLETE

Page 32

Mira closed the first case and placed the completed map in the workshop library. A new parcel waited: how can ONE whole have many parts?
