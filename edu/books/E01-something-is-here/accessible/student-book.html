EXPLORE

Can I find nothing?

Mia wondered where to look first.

LOOK

Page 5

Mia opened a box. There was no toy inside.

Is this nothing?

NOTICE

Page 6

The box was present. Mia was looking. The question was happening.

An empty box was not no presented thing.

LISTEN

Page 7

Mia waited for a sound. No one clapped.

Is quiet nothing?

NOTICE

Page 8

Mia was listening. The quiet could be recorded.

Listening and recording were happening.

SAY

Page 9

Mia wrote the word: NOTHING

Did the word make nothing appear?

NOTICE

Page 10

The word was written. The paper was present. Mia had made a record.

The word was not nothing.

ASK

Page 11

Pip asked, 'What if no one shows anything?'

Mia thought carefully.

THINK

Page 12

Then there is no example to point to, say, record or check.

No hidden example has been supplied.

BUILT - DIRECT FORCING

Page 13

Our checking question has two paths.

An example is presented - or no example is presented.

PATH A

Page 14

If an example is presented, the presenting is happening.

So the presented example is an occurrence - not nothing.

PATH B

Page 15

If no example is presented, there is no counterexample to use.

We do not pretend that a hidden example was supplied.

BUILT - COMPLETE PATHS

Page 16

Those are all the ways our checking question can go.

Presented, or not presented.

RESULT

Page 17

Every example we can show, say, record or check is something.

There is no presented counterexample.

THE ROOT THEOREM

Page 18

There is no nothing.

This is the starting point for Smithian Fold Theory.

WHERE THE RULE STOPS

Page 19

Our rule talks about what can be shown, said, recorded or checked.

It does not pretend to describe what no one can present at all.

CHECK TOGETHER 1

Page 20

Put an empty bowl on a table. What is present? What is not inside it?

Say exactly what you can point to or observe.

CHECK TOGETHER 2

Page 21

Listen for one short moment. Did a clap happen? Did listening happen?

A grown-up keeps the moment short and calm.

CHECK TOGETHER 3

Page 22

Draw or tell what you noticed. Ask someone else to check.

A second check may agree or disagree.

KEEP THE RECORD

Page 23

Keep what happened. Do not hide a disagreement.

Honest checks help us learn.

THE END - AND THE BEGINNING
