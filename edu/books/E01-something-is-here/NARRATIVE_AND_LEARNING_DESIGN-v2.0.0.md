# E01 v2.0.0 — narrative and learning design

## Story promise

On the first morning in the Tower of Star Rooms, Mia meets Sol and Tavi. A note asks
them to find “nothing”. They search for it as if it might be a hidden object. Each
place gives them a different experience: a box becomes empty, a bell becomes quiet,
a card stays blank, a teddy is hidden and a shelf is missing its card. With Nori’s
help, they learn that these words do not name one mysterious thing. They tell us
something precise about what is present, absent, heard, marked or seen.

The emotional journey is confusion → playful search → surprise → careful checking →
shared discovery. The mystery is solved by the children’s actions, not by a lecture.

## Child-facing lesson

“Nothing” is not a secret object to find. We can say exactly what is there and what
is not there. Clear words help another person understand what we saw or heard.

## SFT boundary

The story is an Early Years translation of the admitted Foundation boundary around
the Structural One and omission. Objects that remain are still named. An absent
member is not turned into a new object called “nothing”. Formal claim wording and
receipts remain in the adult guide and claim map.

## Thirty-two-page story map

1. Cover: the shut Star Door and the permanent trio.
2. Title, series position and review notice.
3–4. Magical tower introduction; Mia meets Sol and Tavi before breakfast.
5–6. The Star Door stirs; a clear note emoji slides through the slot and Mia finds
   it. The message asks them to find nothing.
7–8. Paper game: find the note among familiar emoji; the five-star trail appears.
9–10. The parcel contains Sol’s teddy. The child traces the teddy out of the box.
11–12. The teddy is outside; the box is empty. The first star shines.
13–14. The friends walk to the bell room and meet Nori, who is listening for a
   sound. One ring, then quiet.
15–16. Listening game: a grown-up makes one sound, then the child points to the
   quiet bell. Quiet is named after the experience.
17–18. In the paper room, Sol reaches for a pencil. The child chooses to add a mark
   or leave a separate sheet blank. The card still exists either way.
19–20. Seven stars/letters form the written word NOTHING. It is a word they can see,
   not a new object.
21–22. Sol’s teddy rolls behind a curtain. The child remembers where it stopped.
23–24. Seek-and-find spread: follow small paw prints and predict the hiding place;
   the page turn reveals the teddy.
25–26. The Star Door opens to two shelves. One has a card and one does not. The
   child points and names exactly what each shelf has.
27–28. Mia reads the note again. The group laughs kindly at their first idea and
   solves the mystery together.
29–30. Whole-spread recap game: match empty, quiet, blank and hidden to four stable
   emoji scenes; narrator explains what the lesson means and why clear words help.
31. The map is shelved; Nori says goodbye; a parcel arrives by the now-established
   tower slide.
32. The parcel asks the next question: “How can one whole have many parts?”

## Paper games

1. Spot the note: point to 📝 among 🗺️, 📘 and 📝.
2. Teddy trail: trace a curved path from 🧸 inside 📦 to the rug, without crossing
   the puddles of starlight.
3. Sound and quiet: an adult rings or taps once; the child shows “sound” with open
   hands, waits, then shows “quiet” with hands together.
4. Blank or marked: choose whether to make one mark on separate paper; compare it
   with the unchanged ⬜ card in the book.
5. Curtain memory: follow paw prints, cover the teddy with a hand, and say whether
   it is gone or hidden.
6. Two shelves: point to what is present and say what is missing.
7. Final matching trail: connect 📦/🔔/⬜/🧸 to empty/quiet/blank/hidden.

## Illustration direction

The art is an original warm, tactile 3D picture-book world with expressive acting,
clear travel between rooms and generous calm space for text. Characters look at and
touch the story object. No character stands in a repeated front-facing game pose.
The exact repeated objects are OpenMoji emoji placed by the deterministic renderer,
not generated into the scene.
