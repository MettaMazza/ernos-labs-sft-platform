# SFT Early Years Picture-Book Standard

Status: binding production standard for the E01–E04 review rebuild

Age range: 3–5 years, with an adult available to read, prompt or join in

## What the books are

The four books form one connected adventure series. A later book may remember an
earlier event and may set up the next journey. Each book must nevertheless be a
complete, satisfying picture book that can be read and enjoyed without opening the
companion game.

The book is not a printout of the game. A page is a story scene, a reveal, a visual
joke, a paper activity or a quiet moment. It must never look like a menu, dialogue
box, progress screen or collection of interface cards.

## Permanent cast and guests

- Mia is the curious child guide. She notices, asks and helps the group put ideas
  into simple words.
- Sol is the eager tryer. He brings warmth and humour, and he is allowed to make a
  useful first mistake.
- Tavi is the careful checker. He looks again without simply giving an answer.
- E01 introduces Nori, the careful listener.
- E02 introduces Pax, the careful builder.
- E03 introduces Vee, the turning-trail keeper.
- E04 introduces Ivo, the friend who likes to check twice.

Only one new guest is introduced in a book. A returning guest appears only when
their earlier way of helping is useful. No character exists only to deliver a
definition.

## Story rules

1. Every book begins with a warm re-entry into the tower and a clear reason for the
   day’s journey.
2. The characters want something concrete before the lesson begins.
3. Every new place is reached on the page. The story never cuts to an unexplained
   room or object.
4. The child experiences a difference before the book names it.
5. A page turn creates curiosity, surprise, relief or a satisfying answer—not a
   mark scheme.
6. The narrator speaks directly to the child only at natural invitation moments
   and at the closing lesson reflection.
7. The end explains, in the child’s language, what happened, what was learned and
   why it helps in everyday life.

## Language rules

- Prefer familiar words and short spoken sentences.
- Say “door”, “slot”, “note”, “box” and “bell” unless one extra detail matters to
  the action.
- Do not use formal SFT vocabulary on child pages before the child has experienced
  the idea. Formal mapping belongs in the adult guide.
- Explain a new word in the same breath: “The box is empty. The teddy is not inside
  it now.”
- Avoid vague scene language, stacked abstractions, passive voice and unexplained
  metaphors.
- Dialogue must sound natural when read aloud.
- Target no more than 35 child-facing words on most story pages and no more than 22
  words in a single activity instruction.

## Illustration and emoji rules

Generated illustration establishes the original world, character acting, emotion,
lighting and story movement. It contains no interface, labels, answer text, logos or
watermarks.

Repeated learning objects use the same large, familiar OpenMoji colour emoji every
time. They are not regenerated, restyled or replaced by similar-looking props.

Canonical repeated-object vocabulary:

| Meaning | Emoji | First-use label |
|---|---:|---|
| note | 📝 | NOTE |
| parcel or box | 📦 | BOX |
| teddy | 🧸 | TEDDY |
| bell | 🔔 | BELL |
| blank card | ⬜ | BLANK CARD |
| pencil | ✏️ | PENCIL |
| door | 🚪 | DOOR |
| star or earned clue | ⭐ | STAR |
| Moon Lantern | 🏮 | MOON LANTERN |
| moon side | 🌙 | MOON |
| sun side | ☀️ | SUN |
| garden leaf | 🍃 | LEAF |
| looking or checking | 🔍 | LOOK AGAIN |

An emoji is introduced with its word above or beside it. After introduction the
same emoji may appear without a label when the meaning is obvious. Repeated objects
must be large enough to distinguish in print and on a phone-sized PDF view.

## Page architecture

- Square 210 mm pages, designed as facing-page spreads.
- 32 pages per student book, including cover, title/imprint and closing page.
- Full-bleed or near-full-page story illustration on most pages.
- Story text sits in calm negative space or a simple paper-shaped text area, never a
  game dialogue panel.
- Activity pages use the whole page as a paper playground.
- No persistent headers, progress bars, stars, buttons, card grids or UI chrome.
- Page numbers stay small and away from activity marks.
- Text is at least 18 pt for story copy and 22 pt for instructions.

## Paper-native mini-games

Each book contains at least five varied activities. A child must be able to play by
pointing, tracing, speaking, moving a finger, clapping, covering, drawing on a
separate sheet, or using the page turn. No instruction may ask the child to tap,
drag, rotate, press, unlock or submit something in a fixed PDF.

Activities must have a genuine choice, observation or small challenge. Wrong first
tries are safe and useful. Answers appear through a later story event or at the end
of the book, not immediately below the question.

## Accessibility and release

- Every illustration has useful alternative text that describes the action and the
  learning information without spoiling a later reveal.
- Colour never carries the only distinction.
- The accessible HTML preserves reading order, headings, activity instructions and
  descriptions.
- The adult guide explains how to read, pause, play each activity, respond to a
  child’s answer and connect the experience to the exact admitted SFT boundary.
- Each review edition receives a new version and preserves every earlier edition.
- Every PDF is rendered to page images and inspected page by page for legibility,
  clipping, accidental UI, unclear object identity, character continuity and
  story/illustration agreement.
- Review editions remain under `edu/`. Nothing moves to `publications/education`
  until Maria explicitly approves the exact final edition.
