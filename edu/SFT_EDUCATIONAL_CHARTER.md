# SFT Educational Charter

Version: planning draft 0.1
Repository boundary: 30 July 2026

## 1. Purpose

The library will make Smithian Fold Theory freely learnable from a child's
first encounter with distinction, parts and patterns through to independent
reconstruction of claim packages, certificates and empirical checks.

The books are educational translations. They do not create scientific
authority, alter a receipt or replace the admission engine.

## 2. Authority order

Every book, teacher guide, workbook, answer guide and digital activity follows
this order:

1. Maria Smith's explicit instruction.
2. [`../CONSTITUTION.md`](../CONSTITUTION.md).
3. Immutable model-admitted receipts and their registered claim packages.
4. The live claim census, categorical ownership and dependency order.
5. Current branch roadmaps and publication gates.
6. Published SFT papers as readable presentations of the admitted record.
7. Earlier SFT work as prior observation and comparison, never imported proof.
8. Conventional curricula, models and textbooks as reference and distinction,
   never as authority over SFT.

If sources disagree, authoring halts at the disputed point until the live
receipt-backed position is reconstructed. A book must not choose the most
convenient version.

## 3. Non-negotiable scientific rules

- Begin the dependency spine with *There is no nothing*, the One, exact
  positive finite count and part, and the Fold.
- Keep the SFT derivational domain exact and finite unless a valid
  depth-independent certificate says otherwise.
- Never introduce numerical zero, negative quantity, irrational proof scalar,
  floating-point equality, completed infinity or an ungenerated continuum as
  a foundational SFT object.
- Use held labels, complement, direction or orientation where ordinary
  notation would introduce a negative sign.
- Generate candidates from stated rules; never hand-pick the desired answer.
- Preserve rejected alternatives, adverse controls, failures, mismatches and
  unavailable observations.
- Keep WHY, DERIVATION and CHECK visibly separate.
- Never describe scaffolding, a demonstration, a unit test or an attractive
  analogy as proof.
- Never turn an application result into a fundamental law.
- Mark a missing closure `OPEN`; do not bridge it with conventional consensus.
- Never edit or work around the frozen admission engine or a protected
  verification authority.

## 4. Required provenance labels

Every substantive statement receives one of these learner-visible labels. The
visual treatment may become simpler for young readers, but the teacher edition
must retain the exact label.

| Label | Meaning in a book |
|---|---|
| `DIRECT FORCING` | Follows from the foundational spine without an empirical premise. |
| `FORWARD FORCING` | Follows from already admitted SFT dependencies. |
| `CONSTITUTIONAL RELATION` | Registered structural rule with its exact scope stated. |
| `OBSERVATIONAL DERIVATION` | A readable relation built from a disclosed observation. |
| `IMPLEMENTATION IDENTITY` | A property of the representation or executable translation. |
| `MEASURED COMPARISON` | Post-derivation comparison with an external observation. |
| `HISTORICAL CORRESPONDENCE` | Comparison with an earlier or conventional account. |
| `FRONTIER / OPEN` | A precise question that has not met the required closure. |
| `APPLICATION` | A bounded use or test of admitted laws; never the source of the law. |

Child-facing shorthand may use icons such as **Built**, **Observed**,
**Compared**, **Still Open** and **Used**, provided the teacher key maps each
icon to the exact provenance class.

## 5. External knowledge rule

External material is allowed only in four controlled locations:

1. **Question framing** — to identify a topic a learner may encounter elsewhere.
2. **Correspondence and distinction** — after the SFT derivation, to compare
   meanings, domains, notation and predictions.
3. **Measurement** — as source-bound post-seal evidence, with agreement,
   mismatch, uncertainty, absence and unavailable tests all retained.
4. **Curriculum navigation** — to show teachers where a book may support a UK
   programme of study or qualification.

External models must never appear in an SFT dependency list. Curriculum maps
belong in appendices or teacher guides and must say **alignment**, not
**scientific validation**.

## 6. Developmental and qualification language

- Ages are guidance bands, not claims about a child's ability.
- Books must support non-linear progression, rereading and extension.
- The 14–16 series is called **GCSE-age** or **GCSE-compatible** until a
  recognised awarding organisation accredits a qualification. It must not
  imply that SFT is itself an approved GCSE.
- The 16–18 series is called **A-level-age** or **post-16** unless formally
  accredited.
- Undergraduate and advanced books state their prerequisites rather than
  assuming institutional status.

## 7. Standard anatomy of a book

Every book contains:

1. scope, audience and prerequisites;
2. a dependency map back to the foundational root;
3. learning outcomes expressed as observable learner actions;
4. a vocabulary ladder with SFT meanings first;
5. chapter sequences ordered by dependency, not by popularity;
6. separate WHY, DERIVATION and CHECK treatments;
7. complete or age-appropriate candidate comparisons;
8. practice in explaining why alternatives fail;
9. a correspondence-and-distinction section after derivation;
10. a measured-evidence section where appropriate;
11. a frontier page listing exact open boundaries;
12. retrieval practice, cumulative review and transfer tasks;
13. an answer and reasoning guide;
14. accessibility descriptions for every visual;
15. a claim-and-receipt map in the teacher or advanced edition; and
16. version, source boundary, licence and change history.

## 8. Assessment rule

Assessment tests what the book teaches, not learner conformity to an external
model. Across the series, learners progress from noticing and sorting to
describing, generating, deriving, checking, comparing and independently
reconstructing.

A correct answer with an invalid dependency route does not receive derivation
credit. A well-reported mismatch or halt may receive full scientific-method
credit. Learners are rewarded for preserving negative evidence and naming the
boundary of a conclusion.

## 9. Safeguarding and high-stakes domains

- Early-years activities use ordinary safe classroom materials and adult
  supervision.
- Chemistry, Biology, Medicine, Earth, Engineering and other practical books
  include explicit risk controls appropriate to the age group.
- Medical books are education, not diagnosis or treatment advice.
- Human-participant activities require consent, privacy and age-appropriate
  ethical review; no child is used to test a clinical or consciousness claim.
- Unobserved physical or chemical predictions are labelled as standing
  predictions, never established classroom facts.
- Social, cultural and consciousness content distinguishes empirical claims,
  interpretation and ethical judgment.

## 10. Open-source and originality rule

The series may learn from the usability patterns of educational publishers—
short explanations, worked examples, retrieval, mixed practice and clear
answer guidance—but must not copy protected text, illustrations, layouts,
questions or branding.

All created text, diagrams, exercises, datasets and code require a recorded
licence and source history. Third-party content is included only where its
licence is compatible and attribution is complete. Open Government Licence
material is treated according to its own terms and never relabelled as SFT
authorship.

## 11. Review gates for each release

A book is ready for public educational release only when:

- every scientific statement maps to a live admitted claim or is marked open;
- every dependency is earlier in the book or explicitly prerequisite;
- external authority has not entered derivation;
- mathematical notation obeys the SFT domain or is clearly quarantined as
  correspondence;
- empirical wording matches the receipt-backed status;
- a subject review and age-level review are complete;
- exercises and answers have been independently checked;
- accessibility and safety review pass;
- the source boundary and update policy are printed; and
- Maria Smith authorises the specific publication action.

## 12. UK framework references used only for calibration

The library's age bands are calibrated against, but not scientifically governed
by, the following public frameworks:

- England: [EYFS statutory framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/early-years-foundation-stage-framework--2)
  and [National Curriculum key stages 1–4](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-framework-for-key-stages-1-to-4/the-national-curriculum-in-england-framework-for-key-stages-1-to-4).
- Wales: [Curriculum for Wales progression guidance](https://hwb.gov.wales/curriculum-for-wales/introduction-to-curriculum-for-wales-guidance).
- Scotland: [Curriculum for Excellence levels](https://education.gov.scot/parentzone/curriculum-in-scotland/curriculum-levels/).
- Higher education: [QAA Subject Benchmark Statements](https://www.qaa.ac.uk/the-quality-code/subject-benchmark-statements)
  as optional expectation references, never SFT content authority.

Nation-specific mappings will be separate supplements so that one system is not
misrepresented as covering the whole United Kingdom.
