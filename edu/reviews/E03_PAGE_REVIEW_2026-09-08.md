## Adult guide: all four pages

Page 1 has run-together metadata, Maria-only credit and unnecessary “agreed turn”, “recurrence” and “two-role” language in the sharing explanation. Page 2 gives mostly correct intended answers but calls the pointing row copied; it also describes a complete turn without clarifying flip-over rather than full rotation. Page 3 usefully distinguishes demonstration from proof and offers a real double-sided card: bring that concrete explanation into the book itself. Page 4 begins with the last accessibility bullet orphaned from the previous page and repeats the wrong copyright credit. Provide practical book/game instructions, optional hidden-code use and more varied read-aloud play.

## Revision priorities

Not publication-ready. This book particularly exhibits the game-screen problem: backgrounds, small sprite characters, large floating quiz panels and repeated static poses. Use close-up picture-book action: Vee handles one tile; the child watches a flip; each repaired section visibly extends the route; friends cross the bridge; the arch changes when reached. Retain stable emoji identities, prediction, error comparison and transfer, but make the pictures carry the lesson. Keep SFT scope explanations in useful adult prose and do not label arbitrary alternating rows formal proof. Joint credit and sentence case must propagate throughout.
